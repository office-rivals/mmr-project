# Electronics: foosball goal sensor

One device per goal. It's built from off-the-shelf modules on one hand-wired perfboard and runs on
3 × AA cells. It sleeps until the table moves, then counts every ball that crosses an IR beam.

## Decisions

### Goal sensor: DC IR break-beam (TSAL6200 → TEFT4300)

A 940 nm IR LED shines across the 40 mm ball channel into a phototransistor. The beam is 17 mm above
the floor, about the centre of the ball (31 mm usual, 31–36 supported: INTERFACES §0; the 31 mm ball's centre is 16.75 above its contact plane). The phototransistor pulls BEAM_RX (D8/GPIO19) LOW while it sees the
LED. When the ball blocks the beam, `R_PU` pulls it HIGH.

**Blocked = HIGH.**

- **Timing.** The ball blocks the beam for about (31 − 3 mm aperture) / v for the smallest ball. That is
  56 ms at 0.5 m/s and 9 ms at 3 m/s. The node's RC alone (220 kΩ × 1 nF) is 0.22 ms, ≈ 0.3 ms to 75 %; the
  phototransistor's storage at this load is not in its datasheet (that is specified at 100 Ω), so **the edge
  times are a bench measurement** (review 2), not a claim. The default `min_block_ms` (5 ms) must sit between
  the measured edge and the 9 ms block.
  An LDR (tens of ms) would be too slow; that rules it out.
- **Why DC and not a modulated carrier?** We first assumed a dark collector. The user reports some
  daylight in the drawer (2026-10-16): that is handled by the beam-health readings and the daylight
  mode `b` (below), not by modulation. A 38 kHz TSSP4038 receiver would need a carrier that keeps
  running in light sleep, on the RC_FAST clock (about ±5 % against the receiver's ±5 % passband), plus
  0.35 mA of its own. The DC beam is one GPIO that is just HIGH. It costs about 1 mA, roughly 5 % of
  battery life, which lead approved.
- **Optical budget at about 56 mm.** That is 40 mm of channel plus the emitter tip 12 mm and the receiver
  tip 4.4 mm behind their Ø3 apertures (`dimensions.md`, mechanical).
  - The TSAL6200 gives I_e = 72 mW/sr at 100 mA typ (40 min), so about 0.72 mW/sr per mA.
  - At I_F = 1.0 mA, the irradiance is E_e = 0.72 / 5.6² ≈ 0.023 mW/cm².
  - The TEFT4300 gives I_ca = 3.2 mA per mW/cm² typ (0.8 min). That makes I_c ≈ **73 µA typ**.
  - A clean LOW needs I_c × R_PU ≥ 3.3 − V_IL(0.825 V). With 220 kΩ, that is ≥ 11 µA, so the
    **typical margin is 6.5×**.
  - With both parts at their datasheet minimum, I_c is about 10.2 µA against 11.25 µA needed: **0.91×,
    a miss**. The TSAL6200's intensity is only guaranteed at 100 mA (scaling to 1 mA is an estimate),
    and the TEFT4300's light current is specified at V_CE = 5 V. That is why the two resistors are
    socketed calibration knobs (below), and the first-table order carries 470 Ω / 1 k / 100 k / 470 k
    alternatives plus a spare optical pair. BEAM_RX is counted on D8/GPIO19 and read by the ADC on
    D2 (beam health, 2026-10-16): the sockets set the starting point, the server-selected beam mode
    (below) is the remote knob. ASSEMBLY §6 records the fitted values per sled.
- **When blocked.** Dark current is ≤ 200 nA, which drops ≤ 44 mV across 220 kΩ, so BEAM_RX stays a
  clean HIGH. With its tip 4.4 mm behind a Ø3 aperture, the receiver sees about ±19°; the emitter (12 mm
  behind its aperture) sends a ±7° cone. The beam relies on the dark sled/drawer, not on a narrow view:
  the bench checks the blocked level with the room lights on.
- **Receiver polarity (Vishay drawing):** the TEFT4300's **collector is the shorter lead, on the side
  of the flange flat**; the emitter is the longer lead. Collector → BEAM_RX (green), emitter → GND
  (black). **Confirm it with the meter before crimping** (ASSEMBLY §4): diode mode, red on the suspected
  collector, black on the emitter, under a desk lamp: the reading drops well below the dark one; swapped
  probes don't react.
- **Beam health + remote calibration (2026-10-16, lead accepted):** daylight reaches the drawer, so the
  LED and the pull-ups now sit on separate pins and D2 reads the node by ADC. Firmware measures lit,
  ambient (LED off) and ball levels in µA, reports health, and switches between modes from server config:
  `a` (220 k, 1 mA), `a_boost` (220 k, ≈ 6 mA), `b` = daylight (52 k, ≈ 6 mA). Details and the probe:
  [beam-health-2026-10-16.md](beam-health-2026-10-16.md).
- **Daylight margin (review 2, lead's calls).** The old mode `b` (22 k, 4.1 mA) reached only 0.34× of the
  LOW threshold with minimum parts. Now **R_LED_B 270 Ω** (boost) and **R_PU_B 68 kΩ**, with R_LS 47 Ω in
  the LED lead. Minimum-parts estimate (10.2 µA of photocurrent per mA, same caveats as above):

  | Mode | LED | Pull-up | Needed for LOW | Margin, min parts | Ambient that still reads blocked |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | `a` (**the default**, lead 2026-10-18) | ≈ 0.93 mA | 220 k | 11.3 µA | 0.84× (R_LED 1 k: 1.8×) | ≤ 3.75 µA |
  | `a_boost` (per table, only if needed) | ≈ 6.3 mA | 220 k | 11.3 µA | ≈ 5.7× | ≤ 3.75 µA |
  | `b` (daylight) | ≈ 6.3 mA | 52 k | 47.6 µA | ≈ 1.35× | ≤ 15.9 µA (4.2× `a`) |

  - Cost: `a_boost`/`b` add ≈ 5.3 mA while ACTIVE, ≈ +25 mAh/day: **≈ 1.2 months on NiMH while selected**, so a
    table uses them only when its health data asks for it. The worst-case parts are fixed per unit at the bench
    instead: R_LED 1 kΩ (≈ 2 mA, 1.8×) if mode `a` reads `low_margin`.
  - The ambient ceiling is set by LED current, not the pull-up: a clean HIGH and a clean LOW leave
    ambient ≤ ⅓ of the lit current at any R. More daylight margin needs a deeper shroud (mechanical), the
    chopped read (firmware plan C: LED on/off ADC subtraction while `ambient_high` persists, no new
    hardware) or, as plan D (documented, not bought), a TSSP40 modulated receiver.
  - **Bench gate in the real table:** lit, ambient and ball levels in all three modes, worst daylight hour,
    torch through the opening with a ball in the gate.
  - **Health-read ceiling (firmware, 2026-10-18):** the `lit1` check (LED 1 mA, both pull-ups) saturates at
    3.3 V / 52 k ≈ 63 µA, so the measured mode-`a` margin tops out at ≈ 5.6× (fine against `margin_min` 2×), and
    drift detection only sees drops below that ceiling.
- **Calibration knobs.** R_LED, R_LED_B and R_PU sit in sockets (R_PU_B 68 k is fixed).

  | Knob | Start | If the lit level isn't solidly LOW | If response is too slow / noisy |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `R_LED` | 2.2 kΩ (1.0 mA) | 1 kΩ (2.2 mA), then 470 Ω (4.5 mA) | — |
  | `R_PU` | 220 kΩ | 470 kΩ | 100 kΩ (raise I_F to match) |

  Each extra 1 mA of LED current costs about 120 mAh per month (4.7 h of ACTIVE per day).

### Wake sensor: LIS3DH accelerometer (SparkFun SEN-13963 for the first table; clone later)

**Review 2 (lead):** the first table uses the documented **SparkFun SEN-13963** (outline, regulator, pull-ups
and address all known); the clone is a later cost reduction, once a reference unit works. The clone notes
below stay for then.

- **Why an accelerometer and not a spring switch.** It has a tunable threshold and a high-pass filter
  (`MOTION_THRESHOLD_MG` in firmware, 64 mg). It costs 3 µA at 10 Hz in low-power mode (AN3308). It
  catches rod spins and ball hits, which arrive as long bursts. A bare spring switch would also work
  with an RC stretch and a firmware wake gate, but it wears out and can't be tuned:
  [motion-wake-options.md](motion-wake-options.md) keeps that option documented.
- **Interrupt.** INT1 is push-pull, active-HIGH and **latched** (LIR_INT1). It is cleared by reading
  `INT1_SRC`, so a short knock can't slip past the ext1 deep-sleep wake on GPIO1 (an LP GPIO). While
  ACTIVE, the same interrupt is the "still playing" signal for `idle_timeout_s`.
- **Which board (user decision: a cheap clone, ≈ 8 kr instead of 63).** Same chip, same firmware.
  Requirements:
  - The chip is marked **C3H** (LIS3DH). A LIS3DSH has a different register map and address.
  - **No power LED**, or remove it (~1 mA).
  - Any regulator must be bypassable through a 3.3V pin. An AP2112-class LDO costs 55 µA, which is
    why the Adafruit #2809 is out (with its LED, ≈ 190 µA).
  - Pin order varies between clones, so it's wired **by name**: VCC/3.3V, GND, SDA, SCL, INT1 to the
    footprint's holes (ASSEMBLY 3.2).
  - **CS** is bridged to VCC (I²C mode) on the clone. **SDO** is left as the board has it: firmware
    probes 0x19, then 0x18.
  - If the board pulls SDO **down** (0x18), firmware disconnects the chip's internal SA0 pull-up
    (CTRL_REG0 = 0x90) so it doesn't fight the pull-down.
  - The clone's own I²C pull-ups (4.7–10 k) only draw while a line is low.
  - **Acceptance (bench, review 2 split):** **base ≤ 25 µA** with the OLED unplugged and the status blinks
    off (`blinks` 0); then the **full device as an average over ≥ 4 blink cycles** (≈ 40–55 µA expected:
    OLED 5–20 µA asleep + ≈ 12 µA of blinks). It wakes on a brisk ~10° tilt (64 mg) and stays asleep when
    still.
- **Fallback: SparkFun SEN-13963** on the same 12 holes. It is bare (4.7 k I²C pull-ups, 10 k CS and
  SDO pull-ups, address 0x19). Don't bridge its SJ1 (0x18): that ties SDO low against the 10 k
  pull-up and wastes 0.33 mA.
- **Rigid mounting.** The clone is hot-glued flat onto the perfboard (not foam), and the SparkFun is
  soldered on headers. The perfboard is clipped rigidly into the insert.

### Wake latency

- **Deep sleep → ACTIVE.** The C6 boots from deep sleep in about 0.3 s. Players need several seconds
  to serve a ball after they first touch the table, so the device is awake before the first goal.
- **Within ACTIVE, the CPU light-sleeps.** It wakes on any change of the beam pin in well under 1 ms,
  against an 11 ms minimum block. Wi-Fi is off between goals (firmware's strategy), which keeps the
  1–3 s end-to-end budget.

### MCU: Seeed XIAO ESP32-C6

- **Native USB-Serial/JTAG.** There is no USB-UART chip and **no power LED**. The only red LED is the
  charge LED, which lights only on USB. Seeed measures **~15 µA in deep sleep** on the board, at 3.8 V
  on BAT.
- **Pins.** D0–D2 are the only header pins that are LP GPIO (deep-sleep ext1 wake) and the only header
  ADC pins. D0 = battery sense, D1 = accelerometer INT, D2 = the beam's ADC read (beam health,
  2026-10-16). USB sense moved to the MTCK back pad (GPIO6, also LP), as on the scoreboard. The beam is
  counted on D8/GPIO19: it only has to wake the CPU from light sleep, which any GPIO can.
- **Board details that cost current if mishandled.** These were checked in Seeed's KiCad files
  (v1.0, 2024-03-28).
  - **GPIO3** powers the RF switch (LOW = on) and has a fitted 10 k **pull-up**. Holding it LOW costs
    0.33 mA, and firmware holds it LOW for as long as Wi-Fi is associated, i.e. all of ACTIVE. The power
    budget counts it (≈ 1.55 mAh/day).
  - **GPIO14** selects the antenna (5.1 k pull-down = ceramic antenna). Leave it LOW.
  - **GPIO15** is the user LED, active-LOW through 1.5 k. It draws about 0.9 mA when lit and is off
    in production.

### Power: 3 × AA → Schottky → XIAO's own buck (no extra regulator)

- **The XIAO's own power path is built for a battery.** BAT pad → PMOS (on when USB is absent) → an
  SGM6029C **buck converter**: 1.95–5.5 V in, **Iq 2.3 µA**, 1 A. **NiMH or alkaline only** (review 2):
  fresh alkaline is 4.8 V open circuit and a freshly charged Eneloop about 4.35 V. Lithium L91 (5.4 V) sits
  at the buck's limit and over the buzzer's 5 V rating, so it isn't approved (firmware also scales the
  buzzer duty down above 5 V).
- **Why the diode.** The BAT pads also carry the onboard Li-ion charger (SGM40567, 4.2 V CV, 120 mA).
  It runs whenever USB is plugged in and would charge the AA pack: primary cells could leak or vent,
  and NiMH would be trickle-overcharged. The 1N5817 in series blocks that, so **reflashing over USB
  with cells inserted is safe**. The diode drops about 0.15 V at the average current and up to 0.4 V
  on 350 mA Wi-Fi TX peaks. The XIAO's charge LED blinks "no battery" while on USB, which is cosmetic.
- **Why not a separate buck-boost regulator?** The Pololu S7V8F3 would add up to 100 µA of always-on
  quiescent current, 4–5× everything else in sleep. It would also mean back-feeding the XIAO's 3V3
  pin, which Seeed doesn't document as an input, and fighting the onboard buck whenever USB is
  connected.
- **Where the rail drops out (review 2: the whole path).** During a TX burst (≈ 0.28 A) the 3V3 rail sits
  **≈ 0.55–0.7 V below the resting pack**, not 0.4 V:

  | Element | Drop at 0.28 A |
  |---|---|
  | Cells (NiMH, low) | ≈ 0.05 V |
  | FIT0610 springs + its switch (bridged now) | 0.03–0.07 V |
  | F1 2 A pico fuse (≈ 0.05 Ω cold), the slack-loop lead (24 AWG silicone pair) and the JST-PH pair | 0.03–0.05 V |
  | 1N5817 (≤ 0.32 V at 0.1 A, ≤ 0.45 V at 1 A) | ≈ 0.33–0.40 V |
  | XIAO PMOS + SGM6029C at 100 % duty (185 mΩ typ, 310 max, + inductor) | ≈ 0.08–0.13 V |
  | **Total** | **≈ 0.55–0.70 V** |

  **At 3.65 V this predicts 3.65 − (0.55…0.70) = 2.95–3.10 V: it does not meet its own ≥ 3.1 V gate on paper.**
  So 3650 mV is **provisional**; the **bench sets the cutoff** (below), and firmware cuts the burst current below
  the warn level (Wi-Fi TX 15 → 11 dBm, round 3). **D1 stays** (review round 3): an ideal-diode drop-in such as
  the LM66100 does not reliably block the XIAO's 120 mA charger (its reverse turn-off needs more reverse voltage
  than 120 mA develops across it), so none is bought. If the gate needs a much higher cutoff, the months shrink:
  that's the trade, not a part swap.
- **C1, bulk cap** after the diode. It smooths fast (µs) edges and contact bounce. It does **not** carry the
  ms-long TX bursts: 350 mA for 1 ms would drop 0.75 V across 470 µF alone. The UVR1A471MED is end of life,
  and its spec (0.01 CV) is no ≤ 2 µA guarantee: use the current-production part in bom row 7 and screen it
  on the bench (leakage after 10 min at 4 V). **Rule:** fit the lowest of the four; if none is ≤ 5 µA, fit it anyway and
  put its measured leakage into the sleep budget (5 µA ≈ 0.1 mAh/day).
- **Battery drawer lead (lead's calls, 2026-10-18).** The holder rides in a pull-out drawer; its lead (a 24 AWG
  silicone pair, Adafruit 6181) runs in a **slack loop** in mechanical's chamber to a **polarized, latching JST-PH
  pair on J_BAT** (an Adafruit 1131 cut in half), with an **F1 2 A pico fuse** in the + lead at the holder (round 3: the moving lead is always live; F1 clears a hard short, ≥ 4 A within 1 s, but carries 2 A indefinitely, so routing, insulation and strain relief stay the protection against a resistive pinch), so the cells are swapped with the sled in place and nothing has to mate on
  insertion. **Pulling the drawer does not switch the counter off**: unplug J_BAT or take a cell out. The FIT0610's
  slide switch is bridged. Both JST halves are metered before their first mating (cable colours vary). C1 covers µs bounce at the holder springs, not ms gaps during TX: the bench hits
  L with a mallet during Wi-Fi bursts.
- **Battery choice.** The user's own cells; recommended: low-self-discharge NiMH such as **Panasonic
  Eneloop (BK-3MCCE, 1900 mAh min)**. They are
  rechargeable, the user swaps charged sets, and they self-discharge slowly between swaps. Alkaline
  works but lets more go unused below the cutoff and leaks when abandoned. Use matched cells from one
  set: a total-pack threshold can't stop one weak cell reversing.
- **Battery sense.** A 1 MΩ / 1 MΩ divider from the **pack** (before the diode) feeds D0, with 100 nF
  at the pin. That gives VBAT = 2 × V_ADC at ≤ 2.7 µA. The 100 nF is what makes a 1 MΩ source readable
  by the ADC's sampling capacitor. **On USB alone the reading isn't 0:** the charger holds the BAT pad at
  4.2 V and 0.5 µA of D1 leakage into the 2 MΩ already reads as a 1 V "pack". So firmware **ignores VBAT
  while VBUS_S is HIGH** (review 2), and the bench records D0 on USB only (**J_BAT unplugged or cells out**: a
  pulled drawer still connects the pack). Allow ≈ 250 ms of settling
  (100 nF × 0.5 MΩ) after a pack change.

#### Low battery and brown-out

The thresholds are pack voltages read with the radio off (firmware averages 8 samples). **Provisional
(review 2, firmware 2026-10-18): the bench gate sets the cutoff.**

| Threshold | Pack voltage | Action |
|---|---|---|
| `LOW_BATT_WARN_MV` | **3750** (1.25 V/cell) | `low_batt: true` in status; below it, one request at a time and no flash write during TX |
| `LOW_BATT_CUTOFF_MV` | **3650** (1.22 V/cell) | One status, then deep sleep. No Wi-Fi, no flash writes. |
| `LOW_BATT_RESUME_MV` | **3950** | Hysteresis: rested NiMH bounces back about 0.1 V/cell. A swap is a fresh boot anyway. |

- **Why 3650 for now:** the ESP32-C6 needs **3.0–3.6 V** and its in-package flash 2.7 V minimum. With the
  0.55–0.7 V path drop above, a 3.45 V pack dips to ≈ 2.75–2.9 V during TX; 3650 gives ≈ 2.95–3.1 V at 15 dBm,
  less at the 11 dBm firmware uses below the warn level. Firmware starts a goal's chirp before its POST, but a chirp
  can still overlap a POST already running, so the gate includes that case. The brown-out detector (2.76 V) is a
  safety net, not permission to run below 3.0 V.
- **Capacity cost:** NiMH spends roughly 85–90 % of its discharge above 1.22 V/cell; the higher cutoff
  costs ≈ 5–10 % of the months (the budget below already rounds for it).
- **Bench gate (sets the cutoff; warn and resume follow it at +100 / +300 mV in board.h, so cutoff < warn < resume
  always moves together):** the real holder, slack-loop lead and JST-PH in the path (a stiff supply at
  J_BAT hides 0.1–0.2 V); scope 3V3 at the XIAO during a TX burst at **11 dBm** (the below-warn power) with a
  chirp overlapping it, the buzzer at High and the OLED on, plus the final status transmission and a start-up on
  depleted cells; add the calibrated ADC's worst high reading (±20 mV after the two-point calibration, checked
  at 3.6 / 3.65 / 4.5 V); find the pack voltage where 3V3 dips under **3.1 V** and set the cutoff there. **Sweep the whole range at both TX
  powers:** 15 dBm from resume down to warn (the policy above warn), 11 dBm from warn down to the cutoff, the warn
  transition itself, the final status transmission and a depleted start-up. **Qualify the 11 dBm uplink by delivered
  requests, retries and reconnects** in the real table (the counter's received AP RSSI says nothing about how well the AP
  hears it).

## Power budget (fills INTERFACES §3)

This is the derivation behind the goal-counter battery row of INTERFACES §0; other docs cite §0.

Usage assumptions (lead):
- 4 h of play per day, 40 goals per hour per goal, 7 days a week.
- About 8 wakes a day, each with a 5 min idle tail, for 4.7 h ACTIVE per day.

Firmware's strategy (firmware/README *Power*): auto light sleep, with Wi-Fi associated in
power-save during a session and deep sleep between sessions. With the buck at about 90 % efficiency and
the pack at about 3.7 V, battery current ≈ rail current.

Two columns (reviews, 2026-10-15): **typical** uses Espressif-like Wi-Fi and the start resistors;
**pessimistic** uses a busy office AP and the first calibration step (R_LED 1 k). Inputs now include the
RF-switch pull-up, firmware's telemetry and the provisional 3.65 V cutoff. All EST until the bench.

| State / item | Typical | Pessimistic |
|---|---|---|
| **SLEEP** (19.3 h): XIAO 15 + LIS3DH 3 + divider 2 + C1 ~2 µA (base ≤ 25 µA acceptance; OLED and blinks are their own rows) | 22 µA → 0.4 mAh | 30 µA → 0.6 mAh |
| ACTIVE (4.7 h): CPU auto light sleep + Wi-Fi associated, power save | 1.0 mA → 4.7 mAh | 2.0 mA → 9.4 mAh |
| RF-switch pull-up (GPIO3 held LOW while associated: 0.33 mA × 4.7 h) | 1.55 mAh | 1.55 mAh |
| Beam: LED + R_PU | 1.0 mA (2.2 k) → 4.8 mAh | 2.2 mA (1 k) → 10.3 mAh |
| Goal POSTs on the open connection (~0.2 s × 80 mA) × ~160 (40/h at this goal × 4 h) | 0.7 mAh | 0.7 mAh |
| Connect + NTP + config per wake (~2 s × 80 mA) × ~8 | 0.4 mAh | 0.4 mAh |
| Telemetry + heartbeat (firmware README: 300 s flush in production; pessimistic = every flush pays a TLS connect) | 0.6 mAh | 2.4 mAh |
| Buzzer (phase 1): goal chirp 220 ms + undo/scan sounds, ~160/day, ≈ 10–65 mA by duty; button 0 µA released | 0.1 mAh | 0.4 mAh |
| Status OLED (0.91" 128 × 32, user 2026-10-17/18): asleep (0xAE, its LDO + pull-ups) 5 / 20 µA × 24 h + 2 / 10 views × 10 s × ≈ 15 mA | 0.2 mAh | 0.9 mAh |
| Status LEDs (2026-10-18): 30 ms × 2 mA blinks every 5 s asleep / 2 s awake; typical = 1 blink (OK), pessimistic = 2 (attention); + the LP core's blink ticks (firmware, EST) | 0.4 mAh | 0.85 mAh |
| **Total** | **≈ 13.9 mAh/day** | **≈ 27.6 mAh/day** |

Beam mode `a` (the default) is what this table assumes. The beam-health check (every 60 s, ~10 ms awake)
adds ≈ 0.02 mAh/day, inside the rounding. Modes `a_boost` / `b` add ≈ 5.3 mA while selected: for the whole
ACTIVE time that is ≈ +25 mAh/day, ≈ 1.2 months on NiMH (*Daylight margin* above).

| Pack (usable to the **3.65 V** cutoff, review 2) | Typical | Pessimistic |
|---|---|---|
| NiMH 1900 (Eneloop, LADDA), ≈ 1435 mAh above 1.22 V/cell | **≈ 103 days ≈ 3.4 months** | ≈ 52 days ≈ 1.7 months |
| Alkaline, ≈ 1100 mAh above 1.22 V/cell at these loads *est.* | ≈ 2.6 months | ≈ 1.3 months |
| A table in `a_boost`/`b` (+25 mAh/day, only if needed), NiMH | ≈ 37 days ≈ 1.2 months | ≈ 27 days |

- Headline: INTERFACES §0 quotes the NiMH range (mode `a`). Lithium L91 isn't approved (> 5 V on VSYS).
- **The 1-month target needs ≤ 48 mAh/day** (1435 / 30): ≥ 1.7× margin even pessimistic in mode `a`. A table switched to `a_boost`/`b` meets it typically (≈ 37 days) but **not pessimistically** (≈ 27 days); a higher bench-set cutoff shortens all of these.
- **The biggest knobs, in order:** Wi-Fi idle (DTIM, AP behaviour), LED current (calibration), telemetry
  connection reuse (firmware: 300 s flush; a collector that drops idle connections costs ≈ +1.8 mAh/day), play hours.
- The `xiao_c6_per_goal` fallback (reconnect per goal, ~0.2 mA idle) is ≈ +2.5 mAh/day. Measure both.

## Battery voltage accuracy (both boards)

The `low_batt` warning at 3750 mV (≈ 1–2 weeks before the 3650 mV cutoff) needs about ±100 mV at the
pack.

| Error source | At the pack (×2) |
|---|---|
| 1 MΩ / 1 MΩ, 1 % resistors: ratio ±2 % worst case, typically < ±1 % | ±70 mV worst, ±35 typ |
| C6 ADC at 12 dB (ATTEN3) with eFuse calibration (`adc_cali`): **±40 mV at the pin** (Espressif's datasheet, Table 5-6) | ±80 mV |
| ADC pin leakage × 0.5 MΩ source (100 nF hold cap, 8-sample average, radio off) | ±~10–25 mV *est.* |
| **Total, uncalibrated** | **±~100–160 mV worst case** (hence the two-point calibration) |

**Per-unit two-point calibration is required** (review round 3): a bench supply on J_BAT at ≈ 3.6 V and ≈ 4.5 V,
`cal <meter mV>` at each (firmware fits gain and offset, stored in NVS), then a check that the boot log's `mv=` is
within ±20 mV of the meter at 3.6, 3.65 and 4.5 V (ASSEMBLY §5 step 5). Firmware also uses the calibrated ADC API and
averages ≥ 8 samples with the radio off.
- The ±20 mV check is what the low-battery gate budgets; temperature drift is a bench item (cold room once).

# Scoreboard (one per table)

A separate unit on top of the table (one per table): XIAO ESP32-C6 + 4.2" B/W e-paper + 4 soft keys with
RGB LEDs + LIS3DH + speaker (or buzzer) + RC522 card reader + 3 × AA. It talks only to the server (INTERFACES §8).
Why e-paper and not colour: [`scoreboard-display-options.md`](scoreboard-display-options.md) (≈ 190
days vs 5–18 days on AA, before the LEDs, reader and sound; the current figure is in INTERFACES §0).

## Decisions

- **Display: Good Display GDEY042T81** (4.2", 400 × 300, SSD1683, 0.212 mm pixels) on a
  **DESPI-C02** adapter (8-pin 2.54 mm header, 3.3 V, no regulator or level shifter, so it draws
  nothing extra). Partial refresh 0.3–0.4 s (GxEPD2 `GxEPD2_420_GDEY042T81`), full 1.2–2 s. It holds the
  last image at 0 mA and hibernates at ~5 µA. Not the Waveshare module: its RT9193 LDO costs 90 µA
  while awake and its panel revision is uncertain. Not Seeed's XIAO ePaper driver board: it hard-wires
  RST/CS/BUSY to D0–D2, which we need for wake and ADC, and it carries a Li-ion charger.
- **Keys: 4 × MX-style switches** (user's own), hand-wired in a printed plate: one pin of each on the
  KEYS bus, the ladder resistor soldered straight from the other pin to a GND bus wire. Bounce ≤ 5 ms vs
  firmware's 30 ms debounce. Caps and pitch: mechanical. (Was 4 × Omron B3F-4055 on a perfboard strip.)
- **All 4 keys on one pin (D2) as a resistor ladder.** A 100 kΩ pull-up to 3V3, and each key shorts D2
  to GND through 0 / 4.7 k / 10 k / 22 kΩ → **0 / 0.15 / 0.30 / 0.60 V**.
  - Every level is below V_IL (0.83 V), so any key wakes the C6 from deep sleep (ext1, D2 LOW).
  - The ADC (0 dB attenuation, ~0–0.75 V) then tells which key it was.
  - The ladder draws nothing until a key is pressed.
  - Why one pin: the XIAO has only 3 pins that are both LP (deep-sleep wake) and ADC, and every other
    pin is taken by the e-paper and I²C.
  - A 5th key would need ≥ 1 V and wouldn't wake reliably, so 4 it is.
- **Motion wake (LIS3DH) is required.** It's the only way a sleeping scoreboard learns that a match
  started; waking every 30 s to ask the server would cost ~130 mAh/day. Same part, wiring and latched
  INT1 as the goal device.
- **Power: identical to the goal device.** 3 × AA → 1N5817 → XIAO BAT pad, 470 µF, 1 M/1 M sense on D0,
  the same thresholds (provisional: warn 3600 / cutoff 3450 / resume 3800 mV), BOD level 7.
  - **Power can be pulled at any instant** (battery swap). The device holds no match state: a reboot
    re-fetches, and firmware's NVS writes are atomic (INTERFACES §7).
  - The e-paper keeps showing the last image through a power loss. A full refresh on boot clears it
    (ux defines the boot screen).
- **The main board is the goal device's perfboard**, populated without the beam parts, plus an 8-pin
  header to the display and a 100 kΩ pull-up. The keys and their RGB LEDs sit on a separate 90 × 28 mm strip.

## Card reader and key LEDs

- **Reader:** RFID-RC522 module (MFRC522, 13.56 MHz, ISO14443A UIDs: MIFARE Classic/Ultralight,
  NTAG, DESFire). Its power LED is removed, RST is bridged to 3.3V at the module and IRQ is unused.
  Firmware polls it **only while a scan-accepting screen is showing** (Lobby, Teams, Remove-who) and
  otherwise keeps it in soft power-down (≤ 10 µA). The MFRC522 has no low-power card detect, so a tap
  can't wake a sleeping scoreboard. Its transmitter can draw ~100 mA with the field on (peak ≈ 480 mA
  with a Wi-Fi burst): fine on a full pack; near cutoff firmware shouldn't poll during Wi-Fi traffic. The range through the 2.5 mm PETG face is ~2.5–4.5 cm for cards and
  ~1–2 cm for key fobs.
- **Keep-outs around the reader's loop** (edge of the loop copper, any case layout):
  - **NTAG213 sticker ≥ 60 mm** (target; **hard minimum 45 mm**, about 10× a fob's range and 1.5× the
    best card range). Never in front of or behind the loop along its axis.
  - Nothing metal or copper over or under the loop. PCBs and ground planes (DESPI adapter, main board,
    PCA9685) ≥ 10 mm away; e-paper glass ≥ 5 mm and not in front of the loop; AA cells ≥ 20 mm;
    speaker magnet/frame ≥ 25 mm; amp module ≥ 30 mm.
  - Wires (speaker, SD, LED5's 4, the e-paper's) never cross the loop. LED5 sits beside it with its
    wires twisted and leading away.
  - Bench check: ASSEMBLY S6 step 4, the empty-Lobby test (no flash or blip in 10 s).
- **LEDs:** PCA9685 board (Adafruit 815 layout, 220 Ω on every output, power LED removed) drives 5
  Kingbright WP154A4SUREQBFZGW common-cathode RGB LEDs: one above each key, and LED5 below the badge
  scan target. PWM 3n/3n+1/3n+2 = R/G/B of LED n+1 (LED5 = 12/13/14; ch 15 = amp enable for sound option A), totem-pole, HIGH = on. On a 3V3 supply
  through 220 Ω the maximum is R ≈ 6 mA and G/B ≈ 1–2 mA **EST**: Kingbright gives G/B Vf 3.3 V typ /
  4.0–4.1 V max at 20 mA (lower at 1–2 mA), and the PCA9685's output stage drops some of the 3.3 V. So
  green/blue have little guaranteed headroom. **First table: buy 6 (5 + 1 spare), check all 6 player
  colours at the 3.45 V cutoff before buying the other 10**; if G/B are too dim, fit 100 Ω on the G/B
  channels (never remove the limiter). Firmware runs **~1 mA per lit LED** and **≤ 20 mA in total**. Whenever nothing is lit,
  firmware writes every output off and then sets SLEEP (SLEEP alone only stops the oscillator). Common cathode was chosen so that "off" and
  sleep are LOW with the library's defaults.
- **Why not WS2812/SK6812:** their supply minimum is 3.5–3.7 V, VSYS drops to ~3.0 V before cutoff,
  and they would need a data pin plus a power-gate pin that we don't have.

## Sound (INTERFACES §8 Sound)

**Two assembly options on GPIO7 (user decision):** **A, speaker** (the pick): GPIO7 as a PDM DAC →
2-pole RC → PAM8302A clone amp (enable = PCA9685 channel 15, its SD pull-up removed) → 40 mm 8 Ω
speaker; design, sound quality, current and keep-outs in [premium-sound.md](premium-sound.md).
**B, buzzer** (the fallback, below). One 1 kΩ in socket R_A1 or R_B selects the option (ASSEMBLY S2b).
At Medium, A ≈ 12.7 mAh/day ≈ 4.4 months, B ≈ 11.2 ≈ 5.0.

**Option B:** A passive 12 mm magnetic buzzer (CMT-1209-390T, 16 Ω, 2048 Hz, 85–90 dBA @ 10 cm at 3 V), switched
low-side from VSYS by a BC337-40 (1 kΩ base, 100 kΩ base pull-down, 1N5817 flyback). It runs from the
pack, not the 3V3 rail. The notes are 1047–2093 Hz square waves (around the transducer's 2048 Hz
rating), and the volume is their PWM duty. Short pulses scale the fundamental by about sin(π·D), and
the coil's L/R cuts them further. Firmware: the goal counter's `SOUND_VOLUME_DB` in `include/board.h` (the scoreboard firmware was removed 2026-10-18; parked).

| `sound_volume` | Duty (bench-calibrated) | ≈ Level |
|---|---|---|
| 3 High | 50 % | 0 dB (≈ 67–72 dBA at 1 m) |
| 2 Medium (default) | ~12 % | −10 dB |
| 1 Low | ~3 % | −20 dB |
| 0 Off | pin held LOW | silent, 0 µA |

R_SND (socketed, 0 Ω link) sets the ceiling: 10–22 Ω takes 3–6 dB off every level. A louder transducer
drops onto the same driver. An I²S amp would need 3 more pins, and only the 4 back pads are left.
