## Blocker findings

### B1 — The generated wiring diagram has shifted nets, including a dangerous 5 V connection

**Evidence:** `electronics/wiring.yml:82` uses an unquoted YAML flow-list entry `BEAM_RX (J_PT, C_RX)`. The comma makes two entries. The checked-in `wiring.svg` actually renders **BOARD as 19 pins**, including separate `BEAM_RX (J_PT` and `C_RX)` entries. The connection numbers at `wiring.yml:181–183` still assume 18 entries. This is confirmed in the generated output, not just a suspected parser issue.

Examples of the resulting diagram:

| XIAO terminal | Rendered destination | Intended destination |
|---|---|---|
| D4 | BEAM_PWR | SDA |
| 3V3 | SCL | 3V3 |
| D6 / GPIO16 | **5V** | BEAM_PU_B |
| 5V | VBUS_S | 5V |
| MTCK / GPIO6 | ROLE | VBUS_S |
| MTDI / GPIO5 | SND | STAT |

Similarly, `wiring.yml:49` splits `S (STAT, GPIO5)` into two labels: the rendered J_STAT has **four** terminals, whereas `ASSEMBLY.md:157` and the perfboard have three. The red LED lead goes to a bogus third label and RA is left unconnected. The accelerometer's supply and bus wires are shifted too (`wiring.yml:201–203`). Following the diagram's net names can connect GPIO16 to USB VBUS. The schematic/perfboard net definitions do not have this error.

**Fix:** quote these labels, preferably every string in flow lists, regenerate both wiring images, and compare every terminal against `ASSEMBLY.md:259–282` and `firmware/include/board.h`. Add a generation check for 18 BOARD labels, three J_STAT labels and the named endpoints—not merely successful rendering. Do not build from the current wiring diagram.

### B2 — The 3.45 V radio-off cutoff does not guarantee an in-spec rail during transmission

**Evidence:** `electronics/README.md:169–177,195–211` explicitly leaves the cutoff provisional. `ASSEMBLY.md:390` accepts almost 0.2 Ω per contact path. Even using the design's own assumptions at 250 mA, a plausible combination is:

`3.45 − 0.05 (cells) − 0.35 (D1) − 0.25×0.4 (two contact paths) − 0.25×0.185 (buck switch) ≈ 2.90 V`.

This omits further holder, wire, PMOS and inductor losses, and concurrent sound. These terms are an illustrative adverse combination, not a measured waveform. The [SGM6029 datasheet, p.5](https://www.sg-micro.com/rect/assets/3323f5dc-718f-4b81-b820-398ddb702989/SGM6029.pdf) gives 185 mΩ typical / 310 mΩ maximum high-side resistance; a buck cannot recover voltage lost upstream. The [ESP32-C6 datasheet, Tables 5-2 and 5-6](https://www.espressif.com/sites/default/files/documentation/esp32-c6_datasheet_en.pdf) requires 3.0–3.6 V and gives ±40 mV calibrated ADC error at ATTEN3, before the external divider doubles it. A high-reading battery ADC further delays cutoff. The stated 250 mA at 15 dBm is not a guaranteed maximum for the whole assembly.

**Fix:** qualify the complete path at minimum permitted pack voltage, worst accepted contact resistance, representative aged/cold cells, Wi-Fi TX, OLED on and maximum sound together. Require **≥3.1 V at the XIAO rail** as an engineering margin, including the final low-battery transmission and startup with depleted cells. Set cutoff from that result and calibrate the battery reading against a meter. If the required cutoff consumes too much NiMH capacity, change the power path before freezing hardware: use a specified low-loss reverse-blocking solution that still prevents charger current into the pack, or a properly isolated buck-boost supply. Do not simply bypass D1 or parallel another regulator onto 3V3. Recalculate runtime after the change.

### B3 — Daylight mode improves ambient tolerance by sacrificing most of the already weak minimum optical margin

**Evidence:** `electronics/README.md:61–86` admits default mode's minimum-parts margin is 0.91×. The [TSAL6200 datasheet, p.2](https://www.vishay.com/docs/81010/tsal6200.pdf) specifies 40 mW/sr minimum at 100 mA; [TEFT4300, p.2](https://www.vishay.com/docs/81549/teft4300.pdf) specifies 0.8 mA minimum at 1 mW/cm², VCE=5 V. Using the design's optimistic linear scaling and 56 mm distance:

`Ireceiver,min ≈ (40 × 0.001/0.1)/5.6² × 0.8 mA = 10.2 µA`.

At 3.3 V the digital LOW limit is 0.825 V. Mode `a` therefore needs 11.25 µA. Mode `b` has 220 kΩ || 22 kΩ = 20 kΩ and needs **123.75 µA**, but its 4.1 mA emitter yields only **41.8 µA** by the same estimate: **0.34× margin**, even without misalignment or dirt. It would need about 12.1 mA merely to reach that threshold under these assumptions. The suggested 470 Ω main resistor plus 680 Ω boost gives only about 7.9 mA at VF≈1.1 V. Actual guarantees are weaker because the emitter is characterized at a different current and the receiver at a different collector voltage.

The blocked-state ambient ceiling for guaranteed HIGH is only `(3.3−2.475)/220k = 3.75 µA`, or 41.25 µA in mode `b`. Thus the claimed elevenfold daylight improvement is real, but it is not a complete operating margin. An ADC health check can report failure; it cannot restore missing optical contrast. The 1 nF capacitor alone gives a 220 µs RC constant and about 305 µs to reach 75% of the rail, before phototransistor storage and cable effects. The datasheet's microsecond switching specification uses a 100 Ω load, not this circuit.

**Fix:** establish measured lit/blocked/LED-off limits for all supported modes, all actual ball types, low battery, aperture misalignment and the brightest real drawer conditions. Require the guide's <0.4 V lit / >2.9 V blocked at nominal rail, proportionally suitable margins at low rail, and adequate pulse width at maximum ball speed. Freeze a resistor/emitter-drive range that can meet both limits without excessive GPIO current. If it cannot, deepen the optical shroud or use a carrier-based light-barrier receiver; do not buy multiple units on the typical 6.5× calculation.

### B4 — Several fit-critical parts are still descriptions, not orderable, frozen variants

**Evidence:** `electronics/bom.md:34,37,40,206–207` leaves the clone, MX manufacturer, pogo part, OLED module and status LEDs open. In particular, “pick the part at order” does not establish pogo geometry. `mechanical/sled.py:44,56–57,137–138,178–189` assumes a 5 mm wall, Ø2.5 holes, 1.5 mm free projection and 0.75 mm compression. **Total travel is not free tip projection from a seating collar.** The mid-stroke assertion at `sled.py:387–391` checks assumed geometry, not any purchased pin's drawing or force curve. At the specified minimum force, two pins already push back with at least 1.2 N; no latch-force margin is specified.

The OLED's 38×12 envelope and active-area offset are assumptions (`dimensions.md:79`, `sled.py:64–65`). SSD1306 and SSD1315 are not a purchasing equivalence without validating the actual module, supply circuitry and initialization. The clone also lacks a guaranteed outline or regulator/pull-up implementation. Current stock, price and delivery claims were not independently established by this datasheet review.

**Fix:** attach exact manufacturer/order codes or frozen seller variants and dimensional drawings/photos before ordering. For pogo pins record collar-to-tip dimensions, working travel, minimum force at the actual compression, resistance, current rating, plating and solder-cup wire size. Reconcile those with the stop and latch tolerances. Prefer matching plated target contacts over unidentified nickel strip. Freeze one OLED controller, glass/PCB outline, header orientation and pin order; one MX switch variant; and LED low-current specifications. Choose a documented accelerometer module for the first prototype if the clone cannot be specified. Any substitute requires a new fit/pinout check.

### B5 — The first-table cart is not sufficient as a literal build order

**Evidence:** `bom.md:79–100` lacks a 220 Ω resistor row, while `ASSEMBLY.md:158` needs one per counter. The cart supplies three 3-pin housings, but two counters require **four**: two receiver plugs plus two J_STAT plugs (`ASSEMBLY.md:341,348`). Two 4-pin J_OLED housings are required by `ASSEMBLY.md:347` but are not explicitly listed. The updated design uses **19 jumper wires per counter**, correctly stated at `bom.md:39,201`; other instructions still say six, ten or sixteen (`bom.md:72,96`; `ASSEMBLY.md:82,333`). Ten machined socket positions per counter consume the whole 20-position strip, with no spare. There are two 680 Ω resistors per counter, not just the beam-boost pair. `ASSEMBLY.md:75–83` omits numerous later-added parts. A bench supply and oscilloscope needed to resolve B2/B3 are absent from the tool checklist.

**Fix:** publish one goal-counter-only counted cart. Minimum for two units includes 38 jumpers, four 3-pin housings, two 4-pin housings, ten 2-pin housings, 20 machined socket contacts, 40 ordinary male header positions including the two keyed mouth headers, two 220 Ω and four 680 Ω resistors, plus deliberate spares. Count XIAO/module headers separately and confirm what ships with them. Include the remaining per-board passives, protection additions and test-equipment access. Replace the stale assembly parts checklist rather than making the buyer combine amendments.

## Should-fix findings

### S1 — The SparkFun fallback overlaps the added buzzer components in the perfboard plan

**Evidence:** From `perfboard.py:17,35,53–54,256–257`, the drawn SparkFun PCB occupies approximately x=32.47–55.33, y=9.75–22.45 mm. Q2's middle lead is at **(54.06,18.64)**; upright R_BD is at **x=54.06, y=13.56–16.10**. Both lie inside that outline. The top-view render visibly overlays Q2 on the module. `ASSEMBLY.md:202–213` gives neither an exclusion for the clone nor a height arrangement that proves the fallback clears these components.

**Fix:** move the buzzer components outside the module envelope, or specify and verify a deliberate insulating/height arrangement within the 14 mm ceiling. Specify the clone's maximum footprint too. Do not treat the fallback as an interchangeable assembly until its body clearances pass, not just its pins.

### S2 — Exposed battery targets have no short-circuit protection; D1 is not complete protection

**Evidence:** `ASSEMBLY.md:382–393` connects the holder directly to two exposed 8×8 mm targets, 10 mm apart. Their edge separation is only 2 mm. A conductive object across the removed drawer's targets bypasses D1 entirely. No fuse appears in the power circuit (`schematic.py:314–338`). With reversed J_BAT polarity, the pre-diode 1 MΩ/1 MΩ divider would try to put approximately −VPACK/2 on D0; D1 does not isolate this path. Current is limited to a few µA, so this is not a prediction of immediate destruction, but “nothing is damaged” is not demonstrated.

**Fix:** add a suitably rated low-resistance fuse in the holder positive lead, before the targets, and recess/shroud the live targets. Check fuse/contact resistance against B2. Key J_BAT and protect or relocate the sense input so reverse insertion does not rely on undocumented GPIO injection tolerance. Verify reverse behavior with a current-limited source, not AA fault current. Use matched, healthy cells; a total-pack threshold does not guarantee that an individual weak cell cannot reverse.

### S3 — USB isolation is directionally correct, but “dead pins” and USB-only sound need qualification

**Evidence:** `README.md:160–182` claims USB with cells is safe and the exposed pogo pins are dead. D1's orientation does block the intended charging current. The [Seeed power schematic, sheet 3](https://files.seeedstudio.com/wiki/SeeedStudio-XIAO-ESP32C6/XIAO_ESP32_C6_v1.0_SCH_260114.pdf) places the 120 mA charger on BAT; the project's VSYS/buzzer is attached to that battery node, not a guaranteed USB accessory supply. A [1N5817](https://www.vishay.com/docs/88525/1n5817.pdf) has reverse leakage, so “dead” is stronger than the circuit establishes. Leakage at this low reverse voltage is likely small but must be measured rather than equated with zero.

**Fix:** measure signed pack current during USB insertion, normal operation and sound with charged/depleted cells, and measure contact voltage/current with the drawer absent. Scope VSYS on USB-only sound. If charger cycling makes sound unreliable, mute sound on USB or supply it from a proper switched system rail. Preserve D1's blocking function. NiMH charging remains in the external charger. Record the bought XIAO revision.

### S4 — Sleep-current acceptance contradicts the populated hardware and blink behavior

**Evidence:** `README.md:231,239–240` separately budgets 22 µA baseline, 5–20 µA OLED sleep and periodic LEDs. At the stated 2 mA, one 30 ms pulse every five seconds adds **12 µA average**, two add 24 µA, before LP-core overhead. A normal complete assembly can therefore exceed the ≤30 µA limit in `ASSEMBLY.md:498` without a defective clone. The named [Nichicon UVR1A471MED](https://www.nichicon.co.jp/products/pdfs/uvr.pdf) has a 47 µA maximum leakage specification after two minutes at rated voltage; it does not guarantee the BOM's ≤2 µA after ten minutes at operating voltage. Lower actual leakage is plausible, not guaranteed.

**Fix:** separate a steady baseline measurement with blink disabled from time-integrated production sleep current. Measure OLED, capacitor and clone contributions independently. Retain ≤2 µA only as a screened-part acceptance condition with a fallback part, or budget the actual qualified leakage. Validate battery life from a representative daily charge measurement and measured usable capacity at the final cutoff. Do not reject a good clone just because the OLED/blinks are drawing current.

### S5 — Set the shared I²C bus to a supported electrical speed, not merely one that avoids visible errors

**Evidence:** `board.h:32–33` admits a 300–400 ns rise-time estimate at 400 kHz. [NXP UM10204](https://cache.nxp.com/docs/en/user-guide/UM10204.pdf) permits 300 ns for Fast-mode, versus 1000 ns for Standard-mode. At 4.7 kΩ, the RC estimate `tr≈0.8473RC` allows only about 75 pF for 300 ns. The OLED uses whole 30 cm wires despite a shorter nominal route (`ASSEMBLY.md:347`), and parallel pull-ups are module-dependent. Bus trouble can also break motion-wake configuration.

**Fix:** default to 100 kHz for this status display, then measure rise time and LOW voltage with both exact modules attached. Verify pull-ups go to the supplied 3.3 V rail, including a regulator-bypassed clone. Freeze CS to that powered rail, and define SA0 rather than assuming every clone's floating-pin implementation is identical. Address sharing itself is fine: 0x18/0x19 versus 0x3C. [LIS3DH pin description](https://www.st.com/resource/en/datasheet/cd00274221.pdf) confirms CS HIGH selects I²C and SA0 selects the low address bit.

### S6 — Contact bounce, ESD and connector retention are not solved by the bulk capacitor

**Evidence:** The receiver has roughly 0.48 m of cable plus two connectors (`wiring.yml:104–116`), directly connected to two MCU inputs with a 220 kΩ load and 1 nF shunt. The external button's 1 kΩ is at the switch, not at the board (`ASSEMBLY.md:345,395–397`). No connector ESD clamps are shown. Most Dupont plugs are unlatched/unkeyed. `README.md:173–182` acknowledges bounce but the build checklist omits its mechanical stress test. At 350 mA, 470 µF supplies only **0.40 ms** for a 0.3 V drop; it cannot bridge ordinary millisecond contact openings.

**Fix:** add low-leakage input protection and series impedance at the board entry, with an intentional ground-return path; recheck beam timing and leakage afterward. Use keyed/retained plugs or removable strain-relief glue on installed plugs, with harness slack away from the lane. Specify impact and repeated-drawer-insertion tests while transmitting. Do not infer surge immunity from D1 or chip-level ESD ratings. Keep exposed metal and the service opening away from the high-impedance wiring.

### S7 — The assembly documents still describe incompatible mechanical versions

**Evidence:** `mechanical/SLED-ASSEMBLY.md:74,99,195` specifies a 12×12 tactile switch and a front-loaded 45° cartridge. `sled.py:60–63,210–218,257–270` now models an MX switch with a 10 mm printed cap; `dimensions.md:77` instead suggests an 18 mm 1u cap. The model explicitly says an 18 mm cap does not fit. `dimensions.md:48–81` mixes old and replacement placements. `ASSEMBLY.md:94–96,449–450,510` sends the builder to another guide rather than the current sled assembly. The claimed holder fallback is also larger than the modeled FIT0610 (`bom.md:194`; `sled.py:52,159–167`).

**Fix:** make SLED-ASSEMBLY match the MX geometry and actual loading direction; use the modeled custom cap, not an arbitrary 1u replacement. Remove obsolete placement/routing rows and make one current installation sequence authoritative. Prove holder substitutes against tray tabs and lane height. Dry-fit the complete harness and a real USB plug before irreversible soldering/gluing. Model/renders support the overall arrangement, but do not prove purchased-part or printed-tolerance fit.

## Nice improvements and remaining checks

- **N1 — Freeze the small parts rather than “any equivalent.”** `bom.md:41` allows 2N4401 as a BC337 alternative while `ASSEMBLY.md:154` mandates a flat-side orientation. Qualify the replacement's actual C/B/E drawing before fitting; a current/gain rating alone is insufficient. Keep the specified passive transducer: [Same Sky's CMT-1209-390T data](https://www.sameskydevices.com/product/resource/cmt-1209-390t.pdf) rates it at 3 V and 2–5 V operation, 65 mA at its specified drive. The 1 kΩ base drive provides roughly 2.5 mA; scope collector LOW voltage and supply current at the actual peak coil current. Its flyback diode direction in the intended circuit is correct. Do not extend the NiMH approval to fresh lithium AA operation without checking maximum buzzer voltage.
- **N2 — A forward-voltage sum at one current does not prove zero leakage.** `README.md:280` assumes the red/InGaN pair is fully off at Hi-Z. LED junctions have no hard threshold, and no exact LEDs are named. Test off-state current/glow and green visibility at the lowest rail, with GPIO5's input buffer/pulls disabled as appropriate. Keep selected resistor values on each unit's label.
- **N3 — Treat the mechanical/RF environment as a test condition.** `SLED-ASSEMBLY.md:159,188–196` has useful metal clearances but unresolved table measurements. `firmware/README.md:209` already requests RSSI above −75 dBm at 15 dBm TX. Measure with both sleds installed, balls/cells present and people playing, including the two goal ends; clearance alone is not an antenna-performance guarantee. The recessed optics and strain relief are good features. Add a removable shield over the calibration opening for dust/splashes, clean flux around the high-impedance node, and check retention after impacts. No thermal problem is established by the normal average load; check diode/transistor/holder heating during stress operation.

## Pin/net conclusions that do not require a redesign

The intended mapping in `perfboard.py:31–99`, `ASSEMBLY.md:259–282` and `board.h:5–58` is consistent: ADC0 battery, GPIO1 motion, ADC2 plus GPIO19 beam, GPIO21/16 pull-ups, GPIO20/18 emitter, GPIO22/23 I²C, GPIO4 button, GPIO5 status, GPIO6 USB sense, GPIO7 sound and GPIO17 role strap. GPIO0–7 have LP functions, so the selected deep-sleep wake inputs and LP LED output are suitable; GPIO19 is a light-sleep input, not a deep-sleep wake pin. MTMS/MTDI straps affect SDIO timing rather than normal flash boot. USB uses GPIO12/13. These distinctions follow the [ESP32-C6 pin/boot tables](https://www.espressif.com/sites/default/files/documentation/esp32-c6_datasheet_en.pdf); verify normal boots with the button held and after LP-core activity.

The USB divider is nominally 5×100/168=2.976 V; with 1% parts and 4.75–5.25 V USB it is approximately 2.80–3.15 V, a sensible powered input range. Disable its internal pull-up for battery operation. The 1 MΩ divider's 100 nF cap has a 50 ms Thevenin time constant; allow about 250 ms settling after pack changes for accurate readings. Unused beam drive pins must be **high impedance in ACTIVE**, as `board.h:23` states, rather than LOW against the other resistor. These checks do not validate the erroneous WireViz output in B1.

## Challenges: decisions I would change

1. **Prioritize a robust optical channel over remote resistor modes.** First try better physical daylight exclusion while retaining the inexpensive parts. If real daylight defeats that, use a specified light-barrier receiver such as [TSSP40..](https://www.vishay.com/docs/82458/tssp40.pdf), with its correct carrier/burst behavior and a clock that remains valid during sleep. This adds a powered receiver, a third wire and mechanical changes, but gives a conditioned digital signal. It needs qualification too; an arbitrary TV remote receiver is not a substitute. Compare total power with boosted DC operation, not only receiver quiescent current.
2. **Use a documented module for the first accelerometer.** Saving roughly 55 kr on the clone is small beside diagnosing an unknown regulator, address strap or footprint. Keep the clone as a later cost reduction after both variants are proven, and resolve S1 before choosing SparkFun.
3. **Replace the perfboard with a small carrier PCB after one validated prototype.** The six underside XIAO wires and crowded hand-routed nets are a large share of first-build risk. A carrier with named test points, keyed connectors and input protection reduces assembly errors. It adds a board order and layout review; it does not remove the need to validate optics and battery power.
4. **If pogo selection cannot be frozen, simplify the first drawer connection.** A keyed two-wire connector with a retained flexible service loop is less convenient during swaps and needs a protected cable route, but avoids unknown spring geometry, nickel contact behavior and contact-force tolerances. Preserve the drawer only if it passes the loaded contact tests.

## Bench checklist — first unit, before the second

1. **Release the build documents:** corrected wiring diagrams match every intended net; exact variants, quantities, pinouts and physical envelopes are recorded. Inspect diode/capacitor/transistor orientation, all six XIAO pad wires and both module supply rails. Start on a current-limited source; establish normal boot current before fitting AA cells.
2. **Power matrix:** scope 3V3 and BAT/VSYS from fresh cells to cutoff, with simultaneous Wi-Fi, OLED and high sound, real wiring and adverse accepted contact resistance. Require ≥3.1 V at the rail, no reset and accurate ADC cutoff. Test USB-only, battery-only and both; measure reverse pack current and empty-drawer contacts. Check polarity protection using a limited source and verify the fuse arrangement.
3. **Optics in the real table:** log voltages and pulse widths for all modes, LED off, all ball types, slow/fast/off-center rolls, dirty/misaligned apertures and worst daylight. Count at least 100 representative passes, including during health checks, Wi-Fi and OLED activity; no missing, extra or doubled events. Confirm the power cost of the required mode.
4. **Sleep and wake:** confirm motion, button and USB wake, button held at boot, wake after long stillness, and LP status blinking. Measure steady baseline separately from integrated blinking/OLED sleep current. Check I²C rise/LOW levels at the chosen speed and correct LIS3DH identity/address.
5. **Mechanical/contact endurance:** fit the selected MX, cap, holder, OLED and real USB plug. Perform at least 100 drawer cycles and representative table impacts while transmitting; no resets, intermittent beam, pin bottoming, loose targets or pulled wires. Check loaded contact drop before/after and that the lane never pinches a lead.
6. **Installed endurance:** run both table locations through a representative day, with realistic play/idle, AP interruptions and pull-out during queued goals. Record daily mAh, minimum rail, RSSI/retries, false wakes, retained queued events and temperatures. Approve the second build only after every blocker is closed and the selected configuration—not a substitute module—passes.
