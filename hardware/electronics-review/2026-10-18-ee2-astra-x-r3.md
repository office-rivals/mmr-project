**NEW should-fix R3-S1 — The revised battery-harness recipe can produce approximately twice the intended length.**

`ASSEMBLY.md:408–414` cuts the 500 mm extension in half, shortens **only the board half** to 30 mm, then splices a separate 260 mm silicone pair onto the plug half. Literally that leaves approximately **250+260=510 mm** from the battery plug towards the holder joint, versus the roughly 247 mm modeled route and the guide's approximately 260 mm total (`mechanical/reports/2026-10-18-review2-fixes.md:70–71`; `SLED-ASSEMBLY.md:73–74`). The extra PVC lead and splice can enter the rolling bend or need unprovided storage space. General dry-fit advice does not resolve these conflicting cut instructions.

**Fix before cutting:** specify the retained plug-tail length and the **total assembled length between named endpoints**, subtract that tail and splice overlap from the silicone cut, and place the splice entirely in the stationary, strain-relieved section. Only the continuous silicone pair should flex through the U. Verify the actual route before final soldering. This changes preparation, not the selected parts.

**NEW should-fix R3-S2 — The cord model does not prove a taut stop at 125 mm.**

At full withdrawal, `mechanical/sled.py:244–253` leaves the cord's 7 mm-radius semicircle in place even when its upper straight segment reaches zero. The modeled lower straight span is 164.3 mm, so the free cord length is **164.3 + π×7 = 186.29 mm**. The straight distance between those same endpoints is only **√(164.3²+14²)=164.90 mm**. Approximately 21.4 mm remains available to straighten; absent another restraint, that modeled length becomes taut around 146.5 mm travel. The assertion at `:553–559` checks disappearance of the upper segment, not tension.

The physical guide's instruction to tie the cord **taut at 125 mm** (`SLED-ASSEMBLY.md:157–159`) is the right correction, but produces a different, shorter trajectory than the checked model. **Fix:** model that measured taut endpoint length, including actual anchor routing and knots, then recheck its stored path and clearance. Verify physically that the cord stops the drawer with electrical slack remaining and that neither cord nor splice enters a rail. Do not treat the current green assertion as proof of the stop position.

**NEW should-fix R3-S3 — The lower-TX-power qualification must cover both voltage regimes and the uplink.**

The bench instruction at `electronics/README.md:259–263` tests 11 dBm and then says to set the cutoff. But `board.h:12–14,87–89` still has warn=3.75 V and selects low power only below warn. If the measured safe cutoff must rise above 3.75 V, testing 11 dBm at that voltage does not test the 15 dBm state that the existing policy selects. Even with a lower cutoff, the rail immediately above warn must pass at 15 dBm.

**Fix:** qualify the complete voltage sweep, the warn transition, final status and depleted startup. Set **cutoff < warn < resume** with justified hysteresis and account for the measured ADC error around the actual thresholds. Repeat the rail gate for every permitted TX setting, including any later fallback to 15 dBm. The threshold numbers must move together if required.

The RF checklist also equates −75 dBm at 11 dBm TX to roughly −71 dBm at 15 dBm (`firmware/README.md:234`). If this is the counter's normal AP RSSI, that conversion is invalid: it measures received AP signal, not how strongly the AP receives the counter. [Espressif's AP-record RSSI definition](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/stable/esp32c6/api-reference/network/esp_wifi.html#_CPPv416wifi_ap_record_t). Keep receiving RSSI as useful context, but qualify the **11 dBm uplink** with delivered requests, ACK/retry behaviour, reconnect time and charge; use AP-side client RSSI if available. Restoring 15 dBm for link margin also requires its power-path gate.

**NEW should-fix R3-S4 — Finish the fuse installation specification and limit its protection claim.**

The selected fuse is reasonable for this first article: manufacturer nominal resistance is **0.0473 Ω**, or about **13 mV at 0.28 A**, consistent with the new path budget. Its body can be **2.80 mm diameter**, however, while the consumables list provides only 1.5–2.5 mm heat-shrink (`electronics/bom.md:38`; `ASSEMBLY.md:91`) and `ASSEMBLY.md:417` requires tubing over F1. Include suitably sized tubing, for example 4 mm before shrink, and check the finished insulated assembly against the modeled envelope. [Littelfuse 251 datasheet, electrical/dimensional tables](https://www.littelfuse.com/assetdocs/littelfuse_fuse_251_253_datasheet.pdf?assetguid=f47a0bb7-8ede-4679-9646-7114c3787688).

Also replace “a pinch anywhere downstream then blows F1 instead of heating the pack's wires” (`ASSEMBLY.md:417`) with a bounded short-circuit claim. This 2 A fuse can carry rated current for hours; its specified maximum opening time at 4 A is one second. A resistive pinch below the clearing current can still heat locally. Keep the fuse at holder positive and keep the routing/insulation/strain-relief requirements; verify adequate fault-current and wire protection using a controlled fixture, not an uncontrolled AA-pack short. [Littelfuse time-current limits](https://www.littelfuse.com/assetdocs/littelfuse_fuse_251_253_datasheet.pdf?assetguid=f47a0bb7-8ede-4679-9646-7114c3787688).
