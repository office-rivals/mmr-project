**Independent goal-counter hardware review — ee2-astra-x — 2026-10-18**

1. **Verdict: buy after these fixes.** The current package is not ready for a confident first-time build.
2. **First-time success confidence: low, below 50% as documented**; this is engineering judgement, not a measured probability.
3. The drawer targets lack solder/lead clearance, pogo geometry is unspecified, and the documented module alternatives have fit conflicts.
4. Low-battery operation and daylight beam detection lack adequate demonstrated margins; calibration alone does not establish robustness.
5. Resolve the blockers, freeze the actual parts, and qualify one complete sled before duplicating it; the evidence does not support 99–100% assurance today.

Scope: the goal counter only. I read the brief's permitted design, assembly, dimensional and firmware-crosscheck files and manufacturer sources. I did not read design history, dated studies, reports, or other reviewers' files. Local references below are relative to hardware/. This is a document and dimensional review: no physical measurements were made. A finding described as unqualified is not a claim that every sample will fail.

**Blocker B1 — The battery targets cannot be installed as instructed; the pogo fit is not specified by a purchasable part.**

Evidence: ASSEMBLY.md:382–388 requires solder and heat-shrink on the backs of two 0.15 mm targets, then gluing them flush into the drawer. mechanical/sled.py:157–159 makes a 1.2 mm solid rear wall; :181–182 removes only 0.15 mm for each target; :189 fills that depth with the target itself. This leaves 1.05 mm of solid plastic immediately behind each target, with no lead passage or solder pocket. There is no allowance even for adhesive thickness. mechanical/SLED-ASSEMBLY.md:119–120 instead says to press the targets in before soldering, which also heats the plastic and leaves the same inaccessible joint.

The pin problem is separate. mechanical/sled.py:44,57,139,190 assumes a 5 mm wall, Ø2.5 mm holes, 1.5 mm free protrusion and 0.75 mm compression. ASSEMBLY.md:387 seats a collar against the rear face. That requires a particular shoulder-to-tip dimension, approximately 6.5 mm free, plus a defined collar and solder-cup envelope. electronics/bom.md:202 supplies no Mill-Max part number; “stroke ≥1.5 mm, ≥0.6 N at mid-stroke” does not determine any of these dimensions or force at the actual 0.75 mm compression. A longer-stroke pin's midpoint is not necessarily 0.75 mm. The CAD cylinder is not a manufacturer's pin model.

**Fix:** select the exact pin first and dimension the mounting shoulder, retention, free tip, recommended working compression, tolerance stack and lead exit from its drawing. Add rear joint pockets and lead feed-throughs, with strain relief and a retained flat mating face. Allow adhesive without pushing the targets proud. Prefer specified gold mating targets for this unwiped low-voltage connection, or qualify the selected nickel surface through wear and contamination testing. Dry-fit a soldered target and actual pin in a small representative printed section before printing the final drawer. Make both assembly guides describe that same sequence.

**Blocker B2 — The documented fallback parts are not mechanically interchangeable.**

Evidence: electronics/perfboard.py:17,256–259 draws the LIS3DH/fallback PCB over x=32.47…55.33 mm, y=9.75…22.45 mm. Its own placement at :53–54 puts the standing R_BD at x=54.06, y=13.56/16.10 and Q2's collector/base at x=51.52/54.06, y=18.64: inside that board outline. ASSEMBLY.md:153–154 installs these upright parts, then :206–213 specifies the SparkFun board on ordinary headers at the drawn position. No vertical clearance stack is defined to clear the resistor and TO-92 body. A small selected clone might avoid them; the allowed clone envelope and the drawn fallback do not. This is a concrete keep-out conflict, not evidence that a carefully redesigned stack could never fit.

There is also a definite holder-height mismatch. electronics/bom.md:194 says FIT0610 and Keystone 2487 both fit. The stated heights are 17 and 18.5 mm. mechanical/sled.py:44,52,55,162–168 provides a holder base at z=13.2 mm and lane roof at z=31.2 mm. FIT0610 reaches z=30.2; the stated Keystone reaches z=31.7, 0.5 mm into the roof, and conflicts with retention lips positioned for the 17 mm holder. Its extra width also consumes the intended side clearance.

**Fix:** move R_BD/Q2 outside a real module keep-out, or constrain the order to a specific smaller module and remove the unsupported fallback. Check actual component bodies, header heights and underside joints, not only lead holes. Make FIT0610 the only holder for this print, or supply a separately dimensioned Keystone drawer/lane. Do not tell the buyer that the alternatives fit unchanged.

**Blocker B3 — The 3.45 V cutoff has no defensible worst-case rail margin.**

Evidence: electronics/README.md:195–211 and firmware/include/board.h:13 use a radio-off pack measurement and a provisional 3.45 V cutoff. The README's own example budgets 50 mV cell sag and 350 mV diode drop at 250 mA. ASSEMBLY.md:390 accepts almost 0.2 Ω for each target-to-plug path: almost 0.4 Ω round trip, or another 100 mV at 250 mA, before the holder springs/switch and remaining wiring.

The XIAO contains an SGM6029 buck, not a boost converter. Its 100% duty operating mode still has conduction loss; the high-side switch has 185 mΩ typical resistance under its specified test conditions. Using that typical value only to illustrate the missing term gives:

`3.45 − 0.05 − 0.35 − (0.25 × 0.40) − (0.25 × 0.185) ≈ 2.90 V`.

This counterexample uses the design's own current estimate, and excludes additional inductor/mux/wiring loss and buzzer load. It is already below the C6's 3.0 V recommended minimum. The actual current split and regulator loss must be measured; “15 dBm” is not a guaranteed 250 mA ceiling for the assembled product. [Seeed XIAO schematic, power sheet](https://files.seeedstudio.com/wiki/SeeedStudio-XIAO-ESP32C6/XIAO_ESP32_C6_v1.0_SCH_260114.pdf), [SGM6029 electrical characteristics and dropout operation](https://www.sg-micro.com/rect/assets/3323f5dc-718f-4b81-b820-398ddb702989/SGM6029.pdf), [ESP32-C6 electrical characteristics](https://www.espressif.com/sites/default/files/documentation/esp32-c6_datasheet_en.pdf).

Measurement error further reduces the margin. electronics/README.md:318–324 treats calibration as optional. The calibrated ADC table specifies up to ±40 mV at the pin over the attenuation-3 range: ±80 mV referred to the pack. A symmetric pair of 1% divider resistors adds about ±1% to the inferred pack voltage, approximately ±34.5 mV here, rather than the table's ±2%. Averaging does not remove those systematic errors. A positive measurement error can allow TX at a materially lower actual pack voltage. The 100 nF capacitor helps sampling but does not cancel DC leakage or scale error. [ESP32-C6 ADC characteristics](https://www.espressif.com/sites/default/files/documentation/esp32-c6_datasheet_en.pdf).

**Fix:** establish a conservative cutoff from measured loaded 3V3, including measurement error, depleted/cold cells, complete contact resistance, maximum sound and display activity, and aged contacts. Require per-unit voltage comparison near cutoff, or budget the full remaining error. Aim for at least 3.1 V in the qualification test, not a single trace barely above 3.0 V. Replace the two independent 0.2 Ω checks with a whole-path voltage-drop limit derived from that budget, measured under load. Correct the test description to the goal counter's actual loads. If the resulting cutoff sacrifices too much usable capacity, use a true reverse-current-blocking low-drop power path, or a properly integrated buck-boost supply. Do not simply remove D1 or back-feed 3V3 while retaining the onboard supply.

At 350 mA, 470 µF supplies only about 0.27 ms for a 0.2 V fall, before tolerance and ESR. Thus drawer/holder bounce remains a hardware qualification issue; the capacitor cannot guarantee uninterrupted operation through millisecond gaps. Pack-total voltage also cannot guarantee prevention of reversal of one unusually weak cell: use matched cells and check individual end-of-discharge voltages.

**Blocker B4 — The DC optical link is not qualified for the stated daylight environment; mode B trades away too much sensitivity.**

Evidence: electronics/README.md:61–74 already acknowledges that mode A misses a minimum-part calculation. Repeating the calculation for all three modes exposes a worse problem in the proposed daylight fallback (:82–86). The emitter's minimum intensity is 40 mW/sr at 100 mA; the receiver's minimum photocurrent is 0.8 mA at 1 mW/cm² and VCE=5 V. These are not guaranteed specifications at the actual low-current, low-VCE operating point. [TSAL6200 basic characteristics](https://www.vishay.com/docs/81010/tsal6200.pdf), [TEFT4300 basic characteristics](https://www.vishay.com/docs/81549/teft4300.pdf).

Even granting linear extrapolation and perfect alignment over 56 mm, the design's model gives:

| Mode | LED current | Pull-up | Estimated minimum photocurrent | Current needed for LOW at 3.3 V | Ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 1.0 mA | 220 kΩ | 10.2 µA | 11.25 µA | 0.91 |
| A boost | 4.1 mA | 220 kΩ | 41.8 µA | 11.25 µA | 3.72 |
| B | 4.1 mA | 220 kΩ ∥ 22 kΩ = 20 kΩ | 41.8 µA | 123.75 µA | 0.34 |

Calculation: `Iphoto ≈ (0.4 × IF[mA] / 5.6²) × 800 µA`; required current is `(3.3 − 0.825) / R`. This is an estimate, not a guaranteed worst-case transfer model. Aperture loss, misalignment, lower rail voltage, temperature and contamination consume further margin. Raising R_PU improves sensitivity but makes ambient susceptibility and recovery worse.

For a blocked HIGH of at least 2.475 V, allowable ambient photocurrent is only 3.75 µA in A or 41.25 µA in B. The build guide's stronger 2.9 V target permits only 1.82 µA in A. A visible-light filter still passes near-IR daylight. A health reading every 60 s cannot restore a missing blocked edge when illumination holds the collector LOW.

Timing also needs measurement. The receiver's quoted 2/2.3 µs switching figures are specified with a 100 Ω load and 5 mA collector current, not 220 kΩ in saturation. The added 1 nF alone gives a 0.22 ms time constant; storage and wiring add to it. The claimed approximately 2 ms edge bound in electronics/README.md:51–53 is not established by that datasheet. A 31 mm ball at 3 m/s gives only about 9.3 ms geometric interruption, against the stated 5 ms qualification time. [TEFT4300 switching test conditions](https://www.vishay.com/docs/81549/teft4300.pdf).

**Fix:** define a bounded lighting/alignment environment and qualify lit, blocked and transition margins in the real housing at the lowest valid rail voltage. Do not ship mode B as a universal daylight remedy. If retaining DC, choose enough emitter current and shielding to meet BOTH clear and blocked limits across accepted parts, with a stated margin and batch screening; update the runtime budget. Otherwise adopt a fixed-gain modulated light-barrier receiver and a sufficiently stable carrier source, accepting a receiver/mechanical redesign. A comparator can clean up edges but cannot recover a beam with no optical contrast.

**Blocker B5 — The shopping specification is not frozen enough to promise first-time assembly.**

Evidence: electronics/bom.md:191,202,206–207 leaves the LIS3DH board, pogo part, OLED and individual status LEDs generic. “Any” white 0.91-inch OLED can change controller, outline, pin order, supply circuit and off-current. A chip marking does not specify a clone's regulator, pull-ups, CS/SDO bias or board dimensions. ASSEMBLY.md:183 says bridge CS to VCC, then :185 says use a separate 3Vo pin: on such a module CS must reach the actual powered VDD_IO rail, not an unpowered regulator input. The one-GPIO status circuit depends on green LED forward behaviour at small currents, not only wavelength or a 20 mA headline Vf.

There are order-count discrepancies as well. ASSEMBLY.md:338–348 requires **19 jumper wires per counter**, while :333 says 16, electronics/bom.md:72 says 10 and :201's quantity column says 6. Two units therefore consume 38 of a 40-wire pack, leaving only two spare wires. The current circuits require the following minima, before spares:

| Item | One counter | Two counters | Current-list issue |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 220 Ω status-green resistor | 1 | 2 | Absent from the counted resistor table at bom.md:77–100 |
| 680 Ω resistors | 2 | 4 | Boost plus status red consume all four bought; the stated two spares disappear |
| 100 kΩ resistors | 3 | 6 | USB divider, undo pull-up, buzzer base pull-down must all be counted |
| 1 kΩ resistors | 2 | 4 | Button series and buzzer base, plus calibration spares separately |
| 2-pin housings | 5 | 10 | LED, battery, J_PT extension, button, buzzer: no spares in ten |
| 3-pin housings | 2 | 4 | Mouth sensor and status; reconcile the phase-add line with the old three-piece sensor line |
| 4-pin housings | 1 | 2 | Required at J_OLED by ASSEMBLY.md:347, missing from the counted housing table |
| Machined socket contacts | 10 | 20 | Existing 20-contact strip is entirely consumed, not a spare-bearing purchase |
| Male header positions, excluding module headers | 20 | 40 | Includes the three-position mouth key with one pin removed; one strip leaves no spares |

**Fix:** issue one current, goal-only cart with exact manufacturer/board variant, controller, dimensional drawing and quantities plus explicit assembly spares. Freeze the clone's modifications with a photo/schematic and trace-confirmed supply/pull-ups/CS/SDO. Prefer the documented supplier module for the first build once B2 is fixed. Specify the perfboard's actual hole grid and edge offsets, not just its nominal 50 × 70 outline. Keep the specified passive CMT buzzer and identify the BC337 manufacturer/pinout. Pin down a plate-compatible MX variant; trimming the two plastic locating pins of a five-pin switch is acceptable only after its remaining body and tabs fit the modeled plate. Add access to an oscilloscope and an adjustable current-limited supply to the first-unit tool plan; a continuity meter cannot close B3/B4.

**Should-fix S1 — Sleep-current acceptance contradicts the enabled hardware, and C1 leakage is a screening requirement rather than a part guarantee.**

Evidence: electronics/README.md:231 budgets 22 µA before the OLED and status blink. Its :239 adds 5–20 µA OLED standby; :240 and firmware/include/board.h:51–54 add a nominal 2 mA × 30 ms / 5 s = 12 µA per single blink. That is approximately **39–54 µA** asleep, before extra LP-core overhead; double blinks add another 12 µA. ASSEMBLY.md:498 nevertheless rejects a whole clone-equipped device above 30 µA and attributes it to the clone. A slow meter may miss the blinks and make this pass misleadingly.

electronics/bom.md:196 nominates UVR1A471MED while demanding ≤2 µA leakage after ten minutes. Nichicon lists this exact part as **end of life**, 6.3 × 11 mm, 2.5 mm pitch, and 141 µA leakage in its product specification. That leakage rating is not a prediction at this circuit's lower voltage after ten minutes, but it provides no ≤2 µA procurement guarantee. [Nichicon UVR1A471MED](https://www.nichicon.com/en-us/part/uvr1a471med/9154/).

**Fix:** separate a diagnostic baseline with display/blinks disabled from a complete-device average integrated over several five-second cycles. Update the acceptance threshold from that budget. Select a current-production capacitor with a suitable leakage specification, or explicitly purchase spares and screen at actual voltage/temperature. Include measured charge over representative Wi-Fi sessions and reconnect failures before making a months-of-service claim; the budget's 1–2 mA connected Wi-Fi current is still an estimate.

**Should-fix S2 — The I²C timing default starts outside its own stated margin.**

Evidence: firmware/include/board.h:32–33 says 400 kHz with an estimated 300–400 ns rise time. Fast-mode's rise-time limit is 300 ns. With a 5 kΩ effective pull-up, `tr ≈ 0.8473 RC` allows only about 71 pF at 300 ns, shared by both modules, connectors and the OLED lead. The latter is approximately 200 mm in ASSEMBLY.md:347. [NXP UM10204, Fast-mode timing](https://www.nxp.com/docs/en/user-guide/UM10204.pdf).

**Fix:** start at 100 kHz; a 512-byte screen transfer is only roughly 46 ms before overhead. Verify bus levels and 30–70% rise time at the far module, including rail-low operation. Inventory both modules' pull-ups and their supply rails. Keep local decoupling at each module and add it if the chosen clone lacks it. Raise the clock only after measurement, rather than waiting for visible NACKs. A regulator bypass must not leave I²C pull-ups tied to an unpowered VIN.

**Should-fix S3 — Buzzer drive needs peak-current qualification and a USB-only test.**

Evidence: electronics/schematic.py:98–114 drives the magnetic transducer directly from the diode-protected battery node with 1 kΩ base resistance. The CMT has 13–19 Ω winding resistance and a 2–5 V operating range; its 65 mA rating is at 3 V, 2048 Hz and 50% duty, not a universal current limit. The 4.2 V/13 Ω resistance limit is approximately 323 mA; actual PWM peak current depends on winding inductance and flyback. [Same Sky CMT-1209-390T](https://jp.sameskydevices.com/product/resource/cmt-1209-390t.pdf).

Base drive is approximately (3.3−0.8)/1k = 2.5 mA. The BC337-40's high gain is specified in its active region; it does not guarantee low saturation voltage at this pulse current/base drive. The manufacturer's saturation test uses 500 mA collector/50 mA base. This may work, but the present information does not justify treating the switch drop or load as fixed. [onsemi BC337 electrical characteristics](https://www.onsemi.com/pdf/datasheet/bc337-fsc-d.pdf).

The local “VSYS” label is actually the XIAO **BAT pad**, not the board's muxed system-input node. With USB and no cells, the buzzer therefore loads the approximately 120 mA configured charger output and C1. The ESP32 can stay powered while this separate node droops. [Seeed power schematic](https://files.seeedstudio.com/wiki/SeeedStudio-XIAO-ESP32C6/XIAO_ESP32_C6_v1.0_SCH_260114.pdf).

**Fix:** measure coil peaks, Q2 VCE and this node during every sound at fresh/low NiMH and USB-only. Prefer a small MOSFET whose on-resistance is guaranteed at the available gate voltage if Q2 lacks margin; update the footprint. Retain the flyback diode, minimize its current-loop area, twist the remote buzzer pair and keep its return out of the sensitive receiver path. Rename the node VBAT_ISOLATED to prevent power-path mistakes. Limit the approved chemistry to NiMH until the “any chemistry” claim at firmware/include/board.h:8 is checked against the buzzer's 5 V limit and all power inputs.

**Should-fix S4 — A Schottky is not an ideal open circuit: USB-only battery absence is not guaranteed.**

Evidence: electronics/README.md:178–190 says the withdrawn-drawer pins are dead and the battery ADC reads about zero on USB. However D1's reverse leakage feeds a node whose only deliberate load is the 1 MΩ + 1 MΩ divider. Only **0.5 µA** into that 2 MΩ load produces a 1 V apparent pack, the firmware's presence threshold. The 1N5817 datasheet does not guarantee leakage below 0.5 µA at the operating voltage/temperature. Its 1 mA maximum is specified at rated reverse voltage; it must not be misused as a prediction of leakage at 4.2 V. [Vishay 1N5817 reverse-current specification](https://www.vishay.com/docs/88525/1n5817.pdf).

**Fix:** test no-pack ADC voltage and actual reverse current across temperature, with USB connected, rather than assuming zero. Use VBUS detection to avoid relying on this high-impedance reading as conclusive battery presence, or provide a defined presence/load test or suitable low-leakage blocking path and account for its current. Preserve prevention of substantive USB charging of the AA cells. Also qualify reverse polarity with a limited-current source: the pre-diode sense divider remains connected to the MCU, so D1 alone does not prove every net sees zero reverse stress.

**Should-fix S5 — Service connectors and exposed contacts need deliberate fault protection and retention.**

Evidence: ASSEMBLY.md:338–348 uses several mutually compatible, unlatched two-pin plugs. Plugging the battery lead onto J_PT can put raw pack voltage directly on GPIO19/GPIO2; the button's 1 kΩ does not make the whole connector system harmless, contrary to :396–397. The keyed mouth connector is useful but does not protect the other ends. electronics/perfboard.py:73–74 connects the exposed receiver lead directly to two MCU inputs; 1 nF is filtering, not a specified ESD clamp. The drawer's live target faces are upstream of D1, so shorting them bypasses its protection.

**Fix:** give battery power a positively polarized, distinct connector; add positive retention or serviceable strain relief to the remaining plugs and label both ends. Add a suitably rated low-resistance fuse near holder positive and recess/shroud the live targets against incidental bridging, budgeting the fuse's voltage drop. Specify low-leakage input protection and series impedance for exposed sensor wiring, checking that it does not consume the small optical margin. Secure the small XIAO back-pad wires to insulation/board before they leave the module. The target joints must remain insulated after drawer cycling. Verify power loss/restart during deliberate withdrawal; no capacitor of this size promises continued operation. Any copper shield suggested by ASSEMBLY.md:482 needs an insulating layer against the OLED PCB.

**Should-fix S6 — Bring-up instructions permit avoidable damage and false diagnoses.**

Evidence: ASSEMBLY.md:419–425 starts on unrestricted AA power after continuity checks; :456–457 treats absence of visible IR on a phone camera as proof of reversed wiring, although phone filters and a 1 mA emitter can hide valid emission. The :451–453 plug-in list omits J_STAT, although the later checks expect it. Continuity testing alone does not distinguish a reversed component or a wrong rail that happens not to be shorted.

**Fix:** add a first-power stage from a limited-current supply, verify polarity/rails and idle current, then permit full radio load and AA power. Never measure resistance on a powered contact path. Diagnose the emitter from its resistor voltage/current, receiver response and polarity markings before reversing a plug; retain the phone camera only as a positive indication. Include J_STAT explicitly, and require an unpowered continuity/polarity map at each harness end before connecting modules. Measure wire lengths in the complete dry-fit with service slack before cutting or gluing; the guides already identify many lengths as estimates.

**Nice N1 — Close the antenna and table-environment assumptions with the assembled article.**

Evidence: firmware/include/board.h:83 limits TX power, while electronics/dimensions.md's antenna/assembly constraints describe geometry, not link performance. The viewed sled renders cannot establish antenna detuning from installed leads, cells, the table's metal frame or nearby people. Neither a successful bench association nor a wooden enclosure establishes both goal positions' link margin.

**Fix:** qualify both table ends with all panels closed, players present, and the intended AP. Record RSSI, reconnect time, retries and energy, not just one successful POST. If necessary reposition the antenna or use the XIAO's external antenna option with the selector configured correctly. Protect optical apertures from dust while keeping them cleanable; verify the selected black print material's opacity at 940 nm. Exercise the drawer and service plugs repeatedly and inspect contact wear, loosened joints and hot-glue creep. A removable cover for the calibration opening would reduce debris ingress without sealing heat inside.

**Nice N2 — Keep one as-built electrical specification after qualification.**

Evidence: electronics/README.md:282 still describes 3V3 as going only to the LIS3DH, although the net includes OLED, button and status LEDs; firmware/include/board.h:35 still calls GPIO5 spare while :50 assigns it. These are not separate circuit failures, but they make a hobbyist's fault finding harder.

**Fix:** reconcile pin/power tables, physical harness lengths and assembly photographs after the first unit. Record board variants, actual resistor values, capacitor batch, cutoff calibration and accepted optical modes on the unit's build sheet. Remove stale claims that conflict with the goal-only circuit.

| Decision | Alternative I would evaluate | What changes / trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Generic clone and generic OLED for the first article | Fixed vendor variants with schematics and outlines | More initial cost, fewer regulator/pinout/fit surprises. Optimize sourcing after a working reference exists. |
| Daylight handled by DC gain switching | Fixed-gain TSSP4038-class receiver and stable 38 kHz drive, or a sufficiently shielded and qualified DC path | Modulation rejects much steady ambient but still needs sunlight testing. Adds a powered three-pin receiver, carrier generation and a different pocket. | Schottky loss accepted by a provisional cutoff | Keep D1 with a higher validated cutoff, or use genuine reverse-current-blocking power circuitry | Raising cutoff is simplest but reduces runtime. | Custom pogo drawer before contact geometry is proven | Fully specified pogo/target pair, or a retained polarized wired connector on a service loop | Wired connector reduces contact and print-tolerance risk but loses the one-motion disconnect. If that UX is mandatory, retain pogo and finish B1 instead. |

**First-unit bench checklist — complete before assembling the second counter**

1. **Incoming/dry-fit:** identify every module and transistor pinout; measure the actual board, holder, pin shoulder/stroke and target joint. Assemble the complete harness without cells, verify no fouling or pinched wire, and check target retention and remaining spring travel.
2. **Unpowered checks:** diode/capacitor polarity, connector-to-net continuity, absence of unintended bridges, XIAO back-pad insulation, correct resistor values and supply routing. Confirm fuse and contact insulation. Record optical lead identities before trimming.
3. **Limited first power:** supply the battery input through the real path with a current limit; verify BAT node, 3V3, ADC half-voltage and I²C levels. Increase available current for intended radio tests only after inspecting normal idle behaviour. Check cold boot, reset, held undo, USB attach/detach and both wake sources.
4. **Power envelope:** sweep fresh-to-cutoff NiMH-equivalent voltage with representative source resistance; scope 3V3 at the module during TX/reconnect, brightest display and loudest chirps together. Include temperature and ADC error allowance. Require the agreed margin above 3.0 V, and record the resulting cutoff and full-path voltage drop.
5. **USB/isolation:** test USB only, USB plus fresh/depleted cells, drawer removal/insertion and current-limited reversed input. Measure reverse pack current, no-pack ADC voltage and buzzer-node droop. Never connect an ammeter directly across the cells.
6. **Beam:** scope clear/blocked levels and edge/recovery times in A, A boost and B at low rail. Exercise real 31–36 mm balls, slow rolls and hard shots, worst daylight angle, lamp switching, dust/misalignment and simultaneous OLED/buzzer/Wi-Fi activity.
