## Verdict

1. **Buy after one cart fix (new blocker R2-B1).** The battery plug is ordered as "Adafruit 261 … extension cable
   (500 mm), cut in half". Adafruit 261 is a **100 mm cable with one JST-PH connector** ("JST PH 2-Pin Cable – Female
   Connector 100mm"), and two of them can't mate. **Order Adafruit 1131** instead (500 mm, socket on one end, plug
   on the other). Everything else in the cart checks out.
2. **My 19 first-review findings:**
   - 12 closed;
   - 4 moot by the redesign (pogo pins gone, role strap gone);
   - 2 partly closed (S4: the antenna fallback is now hardware and the bench decides; N6: documentation);
   - 1 accepted as is (N7);
   - none still open as a hardware fault.
3. **New from the fixes:**
   - **one blocker** (R2-B1, the cart line above);
   - **two should-fix:**
     - R2-S1: the always-live battery lead now moves with the drawer and has no fuse;
     - R2-S2: round-3 drift. Six documents still describe a keyed Dupont battery plug, pogo pins or the dropped
       drop-in. SLED-ASSEMBLY still says "the lead is the tether".
4. **Confidence it works first time:** **≈ 90 %** with the 1131 swap, **≥ 95 %** with the should-fixes. The remaining
   risk is environmental: daylight in the real table and RSSI inside the table. Both have a fallback (chopped reads,
   the U.FL antenna already in the cart) and a defined bench gate.
5. **The board is electrically clean.** My own netlist check of `perfboard.py` passes: every hole is on one net,
   every part pin is on its intended net, and D7 is free. It matches `schematic.py`, `wiring.yml` and `board.h` pin
   for pin. Every wrong plug is current-limited. Dropping the LM66100 and keeping D1 is right: TI's datasheet only
   blocks above ≈ 0.5 A of reverse current, far above the charger's 120 mA.

## New findings (introduced by the fixes)

### Blocker

**R2-B1. The JST-PH battery connector is the wrong product.**
- bom.md:40 orders "**Adafruit 261** JST-PH 2-pin extension cable (500 mm), cut in half: the board pigtail (J_BAT)
  and the battery lead's plug". The same part is named at ASSEMBLY.md:93, :147, :364, :408, wiring.yml:15, :91 and
  README:225.
- Adafruit's own pages:
  - **261** is "JST PH 2-Pin Cable – Female Connector 100mm": one connector, tinned wires, 26 AWG. Its page links
    product 3814 as the mating version.
  - **1131** is "JST-PH Battery Extension Cable – 500mm": "One end has a JST-PH compatible socket, and the other end
    has a matching plug", 26 AWG.
- Three 261s are three identical single-connector pigtails. Cut in half they don't mate, so the J_BAT connection
  can't be made as written.
- **Fix:**
  - Order **Adafruit 1131 ×3** and cut each in the middle, exactly as ASSEMBLY §4.4 describes. One per counter plus a
    spare.
  - Correct the eight references above.
  - Keep the "meter both halves" step: Adafruit notes it may ship JST-original or compatible connectors.
  - (A 261 + 3814 pair per counter also works.)

### Should-fix

**R2-S1. Fuse the battery lead at the holder.**
- The redesign made the battery lead always live, even with the drawer pulled out (README:226). It moves with every
  drawer cycle, and nothing protects it.
- "No fuse" was decided for the pogo design, where recessed targets were the mitigation and a fuse was said to eat the
  TX margin. That argument is gone.
- A short anywhere between the holder and D1 sees 10–20 A from 3 × NiMH. Ways it can happen:
  - the flat silicone pair's two conductors share a thin web, so one pinch or cut shorts them;
  - a crushed splice behind the holder (it lies loose, ASSEMBLY §4.4);
  - a tool slipping during service.
- **Fix:** a fast fuse in the + wire right at the holder. A 1.5–2 A axial pico fuse runs ≈ 0.04–0.06 Ω cold (check
  the datasheet you pick). That costs ≈ 0.28 A × 0.05 Ω ≈ 14 mV, about 2 % of the 0.55–0.7 V path drop.
- If lead prefers to accept the risk, write that down next to the drop table.

**R2-S2. Round-3 documentation drift: the guides disagree on the battery plug and the drawer stop.**
- A builder following SLED-ASSEMBLY or dimensions.md would make a keyed Dupont battery plug that no longer has a header
  to go on, and would treat the lead as the drawer's stop.
- **Battery plug still described as a keyed 3-pin Dupont plug** in:
  - dimensions.md:19 ("keyed J_BAT (49–54, middle pin pulled)"), :71, :72 ("keyed 3-pin housing on J_BAT");
  - SLED-ASSEMBLY:35, :71;
  - INTERFACES:112;
  - bom.md:72, :274;
  - README:334; schematic.py:319 label; perfboard.py:39 comment.
- **Stop cord missing from SLED-ASSEMBLY:** :161–162 says "Don't pull further: **the lead is the tether**". ASSEMBLY
  §4.4 and sled.py say a separate cord is the stop, never the lead.
- **Pogo pins and the drop-in, both gone, still in the firmware README:** firmware README:191 ("or the drop-in"),
  :236–237 ("real holder, pogo pins and lead … or fit electronics' drop-in"); sled.py comments :10, :105, :138, :499,
  :507.
- **Stale numbers in the electronics README:**
  - block diagram README:27–38 (22 k, 68 k/100 k, R_B 1 k; no R_S8/R_S2/R_BS/R_S/R_LS, LED resistors on the board);
  - README:280 ("3.45 V cutoff");
  - README:297–299 (boost costs 3.1–3.25 mA and 1.9 months) contradicts README:101/:305 (5.3 mA, 1.2 months);
  - README:366 (Keystone 2487).
- **Fix:** one pass, plus a grep check in the existing `check_*.py` for `keyed J_BAT|pogo|drop-in|3.45 V` in the goal
  documents.

### Nice

- **R2-N1. A pinched status-LED lead now shorts 3V3.**
  - With R_R and R_G moved into the lead, J_STAT carries raw 3V3 and GND down the duct. A pinch between the red and
    green wires is a dead 3V3 short. The SGM6029C limits it at 1.58–1.98 A, but the unit browns out and drains the
    pack until fixed.
  - Before, the 900 Ω on the board made that harmless.
  - Optional fix: put R_R back on the board beside J_STAT (top edge, > 20 mm from the antenna). `check_plugs.py` still
    passes.
- **R2-N2. J_BAT's pigtail has no strain relief.** It is soldered straight into two perfboard holes (26 AWG), and
  "unplug J_BAT" is now the service off switch: a latching JST-PH takes a firm pull. Add a dab of hot glue over both
  wires at the board edge, or tie them in the pocket.
- **R2-N3. Set the RSSI gate at the worst-case power.** Below the warn level the firmware drops TX to 11 dBm (−4 dB),
  but the bench criterion is "≥ −75 dBm at 15 dBm" (firmware README). Set the in-table gate at 11 dBm (≈ −71 dBm at
  15 dBm), else fit the FPC antenna.
- **R2-N4. ASSEMBLY nits:**
  - §3.1 writes "**+ −** (J_BAT)" twice;
  - §4's first sentence is duplicated;
  - §9 "The device while the drawer is out" is missing its verb;
  - §5.5's two-point calibration needs the supply on J_BAT's JST pigtail. Say how: the plug half before it's spliced,
    or clips on the pigtail's wires.
- **R2-N5. 100 mA first-power limit.** If the pre-soldered XIAO's factory firmware starts its radio, it can sit at the
  limit and reset. Tell the builder that's expected, and to raise the limit to ~300 mA briefly.

## Bench checklist: first unit (changes from round 1 in bold)

1. ASSEMBLY 3.8 continuity list. **Meter both JST-PH halves before their first mating. Check that no Dupont housing
   makes contact on the pigtail.**
2. First power from the current-limited supply on J_BAT (see R2-N5). 3V3 3.25–3.35 V, nothing warm.
3. **D0 with J_BAT unplugged and USB in** (record it; firmware must report `battery_mv: null`). **With cells connected
   and USB in, no current flows into the pack:** ≤ a few µA through J_BAT, which proves D1 is still doing its job.
4. **Two-point VBAT calibration**, then `mv=` within ±20 mV at 3.6 / 3.65 / 4.5 V.
5. **Rail gate:** real holder, lead, JST-PH (and the fuse, if fitted) and D1 in the path. Run 11 dBm TX overlapping a
   High chirp, OLED on, plus the final status and a start-up on depleted cells. Set the cutoff where 3V3 dips under
   3.1 V.
6. Sleep: base ≤ 25 µA; full device ≈ 40–55 µA averaged over ≥ 4 blinks; the status-LED off-state current.
7. Beam:
   - modes a / a_boost / b in the worst daylight hour, with a torch on a ball in the gate;
   - edges at 220 k and 52 k on the scope;
   - **plan C's 250 µs settling gives a clear-beam difference ≥ 2× its ½ threshold;**
   - 50 match-speed goals including hard shots.
8. False goals: wiggle every plug and socketed resistor; 60 s screen-on with 20 chirps; 10 min of play with no ball
   through the gate.
9. **RSSI in the table at 11 dBm** (R2-N3), drawer in, lid on, both ends; fit the FPC antenna if it misses.
10. **Drawer: 20 out/in cycles.** The loop rolls; the cord (not the lead) stops it at 125; the loop never reaches the
    board or plugs; the JST stays latched; no resets when the drawer is yanked against its stop.
11. I²C rise time before any move from 100 to 400 kHz. Buzzer audible from both ends.
