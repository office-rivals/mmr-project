## New blockers introduced by the fixes

### R2-B1 — The LM66100 purchase is not a qualified charger-blocking “drop-in”

`electronics/bom.md:33` buys LM66100DCKR/PA0087; `README.md:213–217` promises charger blocking. The schematic still contains only D1 (`schematic.py:317–340`): no replacement pin wiring, enable control, local bypassing or physical placement is defined.

TI specifies CE→VOUT for reverse-current operation, a **35 mV typical / 80 mV maximum** turn-off threshold, and **0.5 A typical / 1 A maximum** reverse activation current. Grounding CE does not supply that blocking behavior. [LM66100 datasheet, §§5, 6.5, 8.3.2](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/lm66100.pdf).

**Engineering inference:** with its switch already on, 120 mA through 91 mΩ raises VOUT only ≈11 mV, below the typical turn-off threshold. The XIAO charger is specified at 120 mA in [Seeed's schematic, power sheet](https://files.seeedstudio.com/wiki/SeeedStudio-XIAO-ESP32C6/XIAO_ESP32_C6_v1.0_SCH_260114.pdf). Thus a simple CE→VOUT replacement cannot be assumed to prevent sustained AA charging after USB insertion.

**Fix:** retain D1 until a complete alternative explicitly prevents USB charging, including transition sequencing. Specify all pins, bypass parts, insulation and mounting, then measure signed pack current in both plug-in orders. Do not buy this as an already validated contingency.

### R2-B2 — The frozen Waveshare OLED does not have the described Dupont interface

`ASSEMBLY.md:361,429–430`, `electronics/wiring.yml:46–50,125–130` and `dimensions.md:75` describe four jumpers onto module pins at its end. Waveshare's **SKU 14657** is documented with a **PH2.0 connector** and supplied PH2.0 four-pin cable, not the assumed four ordinary 2.54 mm header pins. Its 36×12.5 outline is confirmed, but that does not establish connector/plug clearance. [Waveshare product specification and package contents](https://www.waveshare.com/product/ai/displays/oled/0.91inch-oled-module.htm).

**Fix:** explicitly use/adapt the supplied cable, identify the actual mating-end pin order and length, and check its connector, bend and service envelope in the dash. Correct the lead-making steps and cart. Verify the active-area offset and panel thickness from the exact module drawing/part. Do not treat a later generic AliExpress module as mechanically or electrically interchangeable without checking it.

### R2-B3 — The new daylight fallback has a sampling blind interval

The allowed firmware fix report (`firmware/review2-fixes-2026-10-18.md:9`) says D8 is ignored in Plan C and the LED-off/on difference is polled every **10 ms**; `board.h:63–66` confirms that interval. The hardware timing requirement in `electronics/README.md:51–55` is `(31−3)/3 = 9.33 ms` for the fastest minimum ball. A full qualifying interruption can fall between samples. A two-sample/minimum-duration detector would require a still tighter bound. This follows from the stated interface; I did not inspect firmware implementation.

There is also an analog limit: with both pull-ups, the available current span is only `3.3/(220k || 68k) ≈ 63.5 µA`. If ambient light saturates the phototransistor with the LED off, both readings can be near zero; subtraction cannot recover lost contrast. The fix report supplies no daylight headroom measurements.

**Fix:** establish a worst-case acquisition interval, including settling and scheduling, that captures the minimum pulse with the required validation samples; phase-sweep fast interruptions under concurrent radio/display activity. Qualify LED-off/on headroom at the brightest allowed ambient. If this circuit cannot maintain it, improve shielding or select the modulated hardware alternative before freezing the cart. Firmware updatability is not evidence that the existing analog channel can be recovered.

### R2-S2 — The moving battery cable is an unspecified mechanical part and the electrical lead is the travel stop

`mechanical/sled.py:59,217–238` models a Ø2.8 mm tube with a **5.6 mm U-bend radius** and fixed path length. `electronics/bom.md:40` specifies only “2-core 24 AWG silicone”; jacket dimensions, strand construction and dynamic bend suitability are not frozen. `mechanical/SLED-ASSEMBLY.md:160–162` explicitly says the **lead is the tether**. The geometric checks cannot establish that a real soft cable rolls into that path or tolerates over-pulling without loading its splices/strain relief.

**Fix:** name the actual flexible cable and confirm its pair dimensions and repeated-bend behavior. Give the drawer a mechanical stop or separate nonconductive service tether so conductors do not arrest travel. Set cut length from the actual routed part, including splices and relief. Qualify at least 100 full cycles and representative over-pull/impact loads while powered; inspect insulation and compare loop resistance before/after. The 10/20-cycle instructions are useful initial fit checks, not endurance evidence.

## First-unit bench checklist before the second build

1. **Document/part release:** one resistor set and connector pinout across cart, schematic, WireViz, perfboard, contract and ASSEMBLY. Dry-fit the exact OLED plus cable, MX/cap, holder, loop and real USB plug. Demonstrate reversal/offset prevention, or current-limited safe failure, before inserting cells.
2. **Power:** calibrate around warn/cutoff/resume; scope ≥3.1 V at the C6 during simultaneous TX + High sound + OLED through the complete holder/loop. 3. **Optics:** record clear/blocked/LED-off voltages and edge/pulse widths in `a`, `a_boost`, `b`, and any enabled fallback, at low rail and worst real daylight, with all actual balls. Verify queued-event behavior when power is actually removed, rather than assuming a wired drawer pull disconnects it.
